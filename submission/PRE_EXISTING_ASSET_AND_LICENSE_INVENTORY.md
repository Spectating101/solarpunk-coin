# Pre-Existing Asset and Licence Inventory

**Status:** founder diligence register — initial version  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Date:** 2026-08-07  
**Issues:** #29, #33  
**Purpose:** preserve the distinction between pre-existing founder work, public open-source assets, research outputs, third-party data, institutional contributions, and future commercial work before any incubator, grant, pilot, company, employment, or collaboration agreement.

This register is not a legal opinion. Exact ownership, university policy, employment obligations, third-party licences, trademark availability, and contract terms require qualified legal review before reliance.

---

## 1. Current repository licence

The repository root contains an MIT licence with the copyright notice:

```text
Copyright (c) 2024 Solarpunk Bitcoin Project
```

The licence permits use, modification, publication, distribution, sublicensing, and sale of the covered software and associated documentation, subject to retention of the notice and licence.

### Required diligence

- confirm which repository files and historical contributions are intended to be covered by the root MIT licence;
- identify files with separate licences or third-party restrictions;
- correct or supplement copyright notices where ownership records require it;
- do not promise exclusivity over assets already released under MIT;
- do not assume MIT grants rights to third-party datasets, trademarks, patents, confidential information, or personal data.

| Asset class | Examples | Current posture | Commercial consequence |

| Research doctrine | ECI, Constrained Ledger, Policy Lab distinctions; evidence/authority/quantity/settlement architecture | founder-originated research programme subject to publication and institutional-rights review | preserve authorship, dated provenance, publication rights, and citation identity |

| Relationships and reputation | source partners, reviewers, institutions, contributors, customers | relational capital, not software IP | protect through contracts, data ethics, attribution, and continuity |

## 3. Founder pre-existing asset record

Before accepting external terms, freeze a dated schedule containing:

- repository names and URLs;
- commit SHAs and release tags;
- package names and versions;
- manuscripts and submission status;
- datasets and licences;
- case and benchmark assets;
- deployed testnet contracts and keys/custody boundaries;
- websites, domains, and accounts;
- brands and visual identities;
- private unpublished designs, source adapters, and operational materials;
- contributor identities and contribution terms;
- known third-party dependencies and obligations.

The schedule should be signed or otherwise preserved with reliable date evidence and updated before each major agreement.

## 4. Third-party and restricted materials

Track separately:

- licensed market or financial data;
- public datasets with attribution or share-alike conditions;
- university-provided data and systems;
- private operator or customer data;
- third-party APIs and terms;
- open-source dependencies and licences;
- external papers, figures, standards, and specifications;
- trademarks, logos, and organization names;
- contributor code or content.

For each material, record:

- source;
- owner or provider;
- licence or agreement;
- permitted use;
- publication and redistribution;
- commercial use;
- attribution;
- retention and deletion;
- derivative-work restrictions;
- expiry or termination;
- contact or authority.

## 5. University and incubator diligence

Before YZU or another institution provides data, facilities, funding, mentorship, staff, or formal incubation, obtain the applicable written policy or agreement and identify:

- ownership of pre-existing IP;
- ownership of work created during the programme;
- university or sponsor licence rights;
- equity, revenue share, overhead, or repayment;
- use of university name, facilities, and personnel;
- student, employee, assistant, or researcher status;
- confidential information;
- data protection;
- publication review and embargo;
- patent filing and cost responsibility;
- conflicts with thesis or academic publication;
- termination and post-programme rights.

Do not infer that “student project” automatically means university-owned or founder-owned. Use the governing written terms.

## 6. Contributor and collaborator policy

Before accepting material contributions, record:

- contributor identity;
- employer or institution where relevant;
- authority to contribute;
- licence granted to the project;
- authorship and acknowledgement;
- confidentiality;
- patent representations where appropriate;
- whether the contribution belongs to public core, research artifact, case-local work, or private commercial layer.

Possible future mechanisms include:

- Developer Certificate of Origin;
- contributor licence agreement;
- employment or contractor assignment;
- research collaboration agreement;
- data-use and publication agreement.

Select the least burdensome mechanism that still supports the intended governance and commercial model.

## 7. Public/open versus private/commercial boundary

### Candidate public core

- doctrine and research papers;
- schemas and generic policy format;
- deterministic evaluation core;
- public verifier;
- controlled fixtures;
- conformance benchmark;
- authorized privacy-safe external cases;
- interoperability documentation.

### Candidate private or paid complements

- private source adapters and connectors;
- customer data and permissions;
- institution-specific policy packs;
- hosted control plane;
- identity/access and administrative workflow;
- deployment, monitoring, support, and service levels;
- implementation and integration;
- confidential reports;
- partner distribution and regulated workflow.

A candidate private asset is not automatically protectable or exclusive. Review derivation from MIT code, contract terms, trade-secret controls, patentability, and employee/contributor rights.

---

## 8. Agreement red flags

Do not accept without specific review:

- assignment of “all present and future” project IP;
- terms that include unrelated founder repositories or research;
- perpetual exclusive licence to pre-existing IP without defined compensation;
- publication veto without a bounded review period;
- automatic ownership based only on incubator participation;
- broad rights to private source data beyond permission;
- equity granted before role, vesting, contribution, and departure terms are defined;
- grant terms inconsistent with open-source obligations;
- warranties that the project is production-ready, legally compliant, or non-infringing without investigation;
- obligations to disclose private keys, credentials, or unrelated confidential materials.

- [ ] qualified legal/accounting advice obtained where stakes justify it.

## 10. Valuation rule

Public MIT code can support a valuable company, but the company cannot honestly value itself on exclusivity over already public code.

Long-term value must compound through:

- original research authority;
- verified external cases;
- trusted brand and founder association;
- private data relationships;
- adapters and integrations;
- hosted operations;
- institutional policies and workflow knowledge;
- customer contracts;
- independent contributors and ecosystem;
- standards or interoperability influence;
- execution capability.

This register exists to preserve those assets rather than accidentally transfer or misrepresent them.
