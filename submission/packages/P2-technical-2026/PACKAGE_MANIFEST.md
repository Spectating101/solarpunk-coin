## 1. Conversion function

Convert CL and Policy Lab into a technical systems contribution that can stand without the full monetary or programme narrative.

## 2. Central question

> How can external evidence, versioned policy, bounded quantity, settlement, and lineage be represented and evaluated deterministically for digital claims?

## 3. Central proposition

> A ledger can preserve records without establishing whether external evidence is admissible, whether an actor is authorized, which quantity ceiling binds, or whether the resulting obligation settles. These functions require explicit policy semantics and reproducible decision receipts.

## 4. Unique contribution

1. separate admission, quantity, and settlement evaluators;
2. deterministic binding-limit attribution;
3. decision identity and lineage across evidence, context, policy, and calculator versions;
4. controlled counterfactual replay;
5. reproducible evaluation of blocking, bounded quantity, and settlement shortfall.

## 6. Route decision test

### Choose ICDLT when

- an eight-page paper can be carried by technical novelty and evaluation;
- related work and baseline/comparison framing are credible;
- multiple edge, failure, or adversarial cases exist;
- reproduction artifacts are stable;
- hybrid presentation and publication obligations remain acceptable.

### Choose BCK when

- the architecture and institutional problem are strong;
- the evaluation remains too limited for a full technical paper;
- a four-page submission can make a bounded contribution honestly;
- in-person Tokyo attendance is financially and practically acceptable if accepted.

### Choose neither when

- the technical story depends mainly on FTSID’s broad institutional argument;
- evaluation artifacts do not reproduce;
- the paper would require unsupported production/security claims.
