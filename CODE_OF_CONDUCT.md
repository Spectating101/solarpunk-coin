# Policy Lab Code of Conduct

Policy Lab is an open research-software project. Participation in repository issues, pull requests, reviews, and other project-managed community spaces is expected to remain professional, evidence-oriented, and safe.

## Expected conduct

Participants should:

- critique claims, code, evidence, methods, and policies rather than attacking people;
- disclose uncertainty and conflicts of interest when they materially affect an evaluation;
- respect privacy, confidentiality, licenses, and source restrictions;
- avoid publishing personal/customer data, private keys, confidential evidence, or sensitive vulnerability details;
- distinguish controlled cases, public-data evidence, counterfactual assurance scenarios, and independently validated evidence;
- avoid harassment, threats, discrimination, stalking, sexualized attention, impersonation, or deliberate intimidation;
- avoid deceptive submissions, fabricated validation, fake institutional endorsements, or manipulation of research evidence;
- follow GitHub's applicable terms and community policies when using GitHub-hosted project spaces.

## Unacceptable content and behavior

The project may remove, hide, close, or reject material that is illegal, abusive, harassing, discriminatory, privacy-invasive, deliberately misleading, spam, malware, credential theft, or otherwise creates an unreasonable safety risk.

The project does not provide a user-to-user messaging or social-network service. Community interaction currently occurs primarily through GitHub. Policy Lab is not designed as a service for children, and it does not provide child-directed community features.

## Reporting conduct problems

For non-sensitive conduct problems, use an appropriate GitHub issue or moderation/reporting feature.

For reports that contain sensitive personal information, security details, or a credible risk of retaliation, do not post the details publicly. Contact the maintainer through a private contact method available on the maintainer's GitHub profile or, when relevant, GitHub's private security-reporting mechanisms. If no private channel is available, open a minimal public issue requesting private contact without including sensitive details.

## Enforcement

The maintainer may moderate project-controlled spaces by requesting changes, hiding/removing material where the platform permits it, closing or locking discussions, rejecting contributions, or restricting participation where necessary.

Moderation decisions should be proportionate to the conduct and should not be used to suppress good-faith methodological criticism or negative evaluation of Policy Lab.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to spaces controlled by the Policy Lab project. Third-party conferences, hosting providers, datasets, or services have their own conduct and moderation rules.
