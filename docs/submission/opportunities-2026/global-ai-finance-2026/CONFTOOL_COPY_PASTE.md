## Primary portal abstract

Financial claims linked to real-world assets or events depend on external evidence, but obtaining or verifying a data object does not by itself determine whether the evidence is sufficient for a financial use, how much underlying quantity it can support, or whether the resulting obligation can settle. Existing oracle, verifiable-credential, proof-of-solvency, and policy-as-code systems address important parts of this chain. This study focuses on the handoff between them.

Using Policy Lab, a deterministic research workbench, we hold one evidence object fixed and vary only the declared policy applied to it. The worked case uses 336 half-hour observations from one de-identified customer in Ausgrid's Solar Home Electricity Data for 1-7 July 2012. For each interval, surplus is calculated as max(PV generation - general load - controlled load, 0), producing 33.066 kWh over the week. This is a derived physical surplus quantity, not a directly metered export channel or monetary valuation. Because the research copy has no source-holder-confirmed custody or operator signature, it remains in the workbench's lowest assurance tier (L0).

Under an open research policy requiring positive surplus and no evidence-level blockers, the evidence-supported quantity is the binding ceiling and the case is admitted up to a claim quantity corresponding to 33.066 kWh. Under a stricter research policy requiring signed evidence and a higher assurance threshold, the identical evidence is blocked before quantity authorization. The policies are research sensitivity configurations rather than calibrated or institutionally endorsed rules.

For the admitted case, a separate settlement stress assumes capacity for only 40% of the declared quantity. Settlement is partial: 13.2264 kWh is covered and 19.8396 kWh remains short. The result shows, within the executable model, that source assurance, policy admission, supported physical quantity, and settlement can fail at different stages. The case does not establish legal issuance authority, monetary value, enforceable redemption, optimal policy thresholds, market adoption, or general external validity.

This study examines how external evidence becomes usable authority for an energy-linked financial claim. Using Policy Lab, a deterministic research workbench, we hold one evidence object fixed while changing the policy applied to it. The case uses 336 half-hour observations from Ausgrid's Solar Home Electricity Data. A conservative surplus calculation produces 33.066 kWh; this is a derived physical quantity, not directly metered export or monetary value. The research copy remains at the workbench's lowest assurance tier because it has no source-holder-confirmed custody or operator signature.

An open research policy admits a claim quantity corresponding to the 33.066 kWh evidence-supported amount. A stricter research policy blocks the identical evidence because it requires signed evidence and a higher assurance threshold. A separate 40% settlement stress then covers 13.2264 kWh and leaves 19.8396 kWh short. The experiment therefore separates four decisions that are easy to conflate: source assurance, policy admission, supported physical quantity, and settlement. The policies are sensitivity configurations, not calibrated or institutionally endorsed rules, and the case does not establish monetary valuation, legal issuance authority, enforceable redemption, optimal policy thresholds, or general external validity.
