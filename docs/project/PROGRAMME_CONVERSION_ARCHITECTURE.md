# Solarpunk Programme Conversion Architecture

**Status:** operational authority  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Date:** 2026-08-05  
**Programme:** Solarpunk Monetary Systems → ECI → Constrained Ledger → Policy Lab  
**Purpose:** convert one authoritative research programme into distinct academic, technical, demonstrator, funding, and software outputs without fragmenting the underlying work.

---

## 1. Core operating principle

The programme is intrinsically valuable before publication, competition results, funding, or institutional adoption. External routes do not create the underlying value. They perform five conversion functions:

1. **verification** — expose claims, methods, and software to external challenge;
2. **legibility** — package the work in forms understood by researchers, judges, funders, and users;
3. **distribution** — place the work in venues where relevant communities can encounter it;
4. **capitalization** — convert credible work into grants, prizes, incubation, pilots, or revenue;
5. **durability** — preserve the work as papers, releases, datasets, demonstrations, and institutional records.

The programme therefore uses the following model:

```text
INTRINSIC PROGRAMME ASSETS
        ↓
CANONICAL SOURCE AND EVIDENCE STACK
        ↓
BOUNDED CONVERSION PACKAGES
        ↓
EXTERNAL ROUTES
        ↓
FEEDBACK / RECOGNITION / FUNDING / ADOPTION
        ↓
REVISED CANONICAL PROGRAMME
```

No venue-specific package may become a competing source of truth.

## 2. Programme identity

### 2.1 Central research problem

> Under what empirical, institutional, and technical conditions may external evidence legitimately authorize a bounded digital or financial claim?

### 2.2 Doctrine

```text
signal ≠ evidence
→ evidence ≠ authority
→ authority ≠ quantity
→ quantity ≠ settlement
→ financial claim ≠ money
```

### 2.3 Programme layers

| Layer | Function | Current role |
|---|---|---|
| **Solarpunk Monetary Systems** | umbrella research programme | frames the long-horizon monetary and institutional question |
| **ECI** | empirical admissibility | tests whether a signal is informative and usable for a declared purpose |
| **Constrained Ledger** | institutional architecture | defines admission, authority, quantity, identity, anti-reuse, settlement, and governance constraints |
| **Policy Lab** | executable research method | evaluates cases under versioned policies and produces deterministic decisions and receipts |
| **SPK** | reference implementation history | preserves the earlier implementation path and technical evidence |

### 2.4 Durable career domain

The career-level identity is not limited to an energy-backed currency.

> **Evidence-gated institutional and financial systems:** how observed reality becomes admissible evidence, bounded authority, accountable quantity, and auditable action.

This domain survives changes in blockchain platforms, energy-money feasibility, specific empirical results, and individual publication outcomes.

## 4. Four substantive workstreams

The portfolio may contain many external routes, but only four substantive workstreams.

### Workstream A — Integrated programme research

**Question:** Under what conditions can energy evidence credibly constrain a digital financial claim?

**Primary output:** FTSID full paper.

**Core assets:**

- backing fallacy;
- ECI admissibility;
- CL institutional closure;
- Norway comparative grounding;
- Policy Lab method;
- controlled flagship sequence;
- explicit monetary non-claims.

### Workstream B — Technical constraint systems

**Question:** How can evidence, policy, quantity limits, identity, settlement, and lineage be represented and evaluated deterministically?

**Primary output:** IEEE ICDLT full paper or BCK26 four-page submission.

**Later outputs:** ICBCT, Ledger, ACM DLT.

**Core assets:**

- object and policy model;
- admission and quantity semantics;
- deterministic identity and receipts;
- counterfactual replay;
- evaluation and related-work comparison;
- adversarial and failure cases.

### Workstream C — Executable climate assurance

**Question:** Can the architecture produce a usable, inspectable assurance workflow for renewable-energy claims?

**Primary output:** IEEE ClimateChain prototype.

**Derivatives:** NTUB FinTech, Global Sustainability Challenge, Blockchain for Good.

**Core assets:**

- evidence lifecycle;
- certificate identity;
- transfer and cancellation;
- anti-reuse;
- correction and revision lineage;
- public repository and demonstration;
- initial user and pilot hypothesis.

### Workstream D — Empirical and software maturation

**Question:** Which programme component is strong enough for a specialized durable publication?

**Conditional outputs:**

- FINEC — result-led ECI econometrics;
- Energy Informatics — energy-information methodology or software;
- JOSS — mature Policy Lab research software;
- Ledger / ACM DLT — mature technical and institutional system.

This workstream begins after the 2026 flagship cycle identifies the strongest validated component.

## 8. Submission integrity rules

1. Every package receives a unique package ID and version.
2. Every central statement maps to a claim-register entry.
3. Every quantitative result maps to a result-ledger entry and reproduction path.
4. Every controlled or modeled input is labeled at first use.
5. Norway is comparative institutional evidence, not endorsement or external validation.
6. Passing admission never implies settlement, circulation, liquidity, or money.
7. Venue-specific wording may change; the underlying evidence status may not.
8. Simultaneous submissions must be checked for substantive overlap before upload.
9. Public competition language may simplify the architecture but may not invent customers, pilots, integrations, or adoption.
10. No platform ecosystem application is permitted without a genuine platform-specific implementation plan.

## 10. Success measures

The programme must not define success only as winning or acceptance.

| Level | Success condition |
|---|---|
| Intrinsic | canonical research, code, evidence, and claims become clearer and more reproducible |
| Submission | compliant, honest, differentiated packages are shipped on time |
| External | acceptances, finalist positions, reviewer feedback, funding interviews, or mentor access |
| Validation | attributable expert review, real data comparison, pilot, or external user testing |
| Durable | peer-reviewed paper, stable software release, documented method, or institutional partnership |
| Commercial | paid pilot, grant-supported deployment, repeat user, or credible procurement pathway |

The portfolio is successful if each conversion produces a durable improvement even when the immediate external result is rejection.
