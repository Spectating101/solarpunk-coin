# Maximum-Value Execution Programme

**Status:** programme execution authority candidate  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Date:** 2026-08-07  
**Programme:** Solarpunk Monetary Systems → ECI → Constrained Ledger → Policy Lab  
**Purpose:** convert the existing research and software foundation into a category-defining body of work, externally validated infrastructure, institutional adoption, durable publications, and an ownership-preserving commercial option.

---

## 1. North star

The programme is not optimized for repository size, feature count, short-term token activity, or one narrow consulting offer.

The maximum-value objective is:

> Define and operationalize the category of evidence-governed claims: systems that keep evidence, authority, admissibility, quantity, settlement, and final claims separate, inspectable, bounded, and reproducible.

Energy is the first proving domain because it combines physical measurements, institutional rules, time-dependent quantities, certificate identity, anti-reuse requirements, settlement questions, and financial consequences.

The programme may later generalize only after the energy proving domain produces attributable external evidence and repeatable results.

---

## 2. What must compound

Maximum value requires six capital systems to reinforce one another.

| Capital system | Programme asset | Required proof |
|---|---|---|
| **Intellectual capital** | doctrine, terminology, formal distinctions, papers | external citation, adoption of concepts, defensible novelty |
| **Technical capital** | deterministic engine, schemas, receipts, verifier, conformance suite | reproducible release, adversarial tests, independent implementation or use |
| **Evidence capital** | external cases, source relationships, reviewed metadata | multiple attributable sources with preserved custody and permission |
| **Institutional capital** | universities, operators, reviewers, contributors, working groups | repeat use by independent organizations and contributors |
| **Commercial capital** | private adapters, deployments, policy packs, support, contracts | paid pilot, recurring use, repeatable onboarding |
| **Reputation capital** | flagship explanations, talks, demonstrations, public record | recognized authorship, invitations, references, visible external outcomes |

No single system may be used to pretend that another has matured. A paper is not adoption. A pilot is not a standard. A large repository is not market validation. A grant headline is not company valuation.

## 3. Programme maturity gates

### M0 — Controlled research system

**Current foundation:** substantial.

Exit evidence already available includes deterministic controlled cases, policy evaluation, decision and settlement receipts, lineage, public interfaces, custody-first source intake, institutional evidence packages, and a tested private-workspace scaffold.

**Remaining boundary:** controlled evidence cannot establish external validity.

### M1 — External operability

**Exit criteria:**

- one attributable and permissioned owner/operator or registry source;
- original artifact SHA-256 and byte length;
- bounded measurement window, timezone, fields, units, and interval semantics;
- registered adapter rather than hidden source-specific logic;
- row, unit, interval, and window diagnostics;
- L0 source evaluation with no automatic assurance promotion;
- open and pilot policy evaluation;
- receipt and Research Capsule verification;
- source-holder factual review and publication-boundary confirmation.

**Canonical gate:** External Case 001 / issue #26.

### M2 — Repeatable external evidence

**Exit criteria:**

- three independent external cases;
- at least two source formats or operational environments;
- at least one correctly blocked result;
- at least one admitted or admitted-with-limit result;
- one source with independently checkable authentication or corroboration;
- adapter effort, manual intervention, unresolved semantics, and reproduction time recorded.

This gate establishes repeatability, not commercial demand.

### M3 — Independent technical and domain scrutiny

**Exit criteria:**

- one energy, sustainability, certification, or market-domain reviewer;
- one software, security, reproducibility, or digital-governance reviewer;
- written review findings;
- corrections or explicit rejection rationale recorded;
- standards-alignment matrix with implemented, partial, configurable, external-authority, out-of-scope, and prohibited-claim labels;
- public threat model and release-integrity baseline.

### M4 — Research authority

**Exit criteria:**

- one definitive integrated methodology paper;
- one technically distinct architecture/evaluation paper or equivalent reviewed output;
- stable archived research release;
- benchmark and conformance corpus;
- claim and result ledgers frozen for the release;
- no unresolved contradiction between manuscript, software, public demo, and case artifacts.

### M5 — Institutional use

**Exit criteria:**

- three independent organizations complete a bounded workflow;
- at least one organization repeats the workflow;
- one external operator can complete a documented path without founder intervention at every step;
- user feedback produces attributable product corrections;
- external contributor or maintainer pathway exists.

Institutional use may be paid or unpaid. It does not by itself establish a scalable business.

### M6 — Commercial proof

**Exit criteria:**

- one paid institutional pilot;
- three serious letters of interest or equivalent procurement evidence;
- one defined initial buyer and budget owner;
- repeatable statement of problem, deliverable, success criteria, and price;
- written treatment of pre-existing IP, future contributions, publication rights, and open-source boundaries;
- evidence that the customer values the result rather than merely supporting student research.

### M7 — Repeatable product and category formation

**Exit criteria:**

- three paying organizations or equivalent recurring contracts;
- standard onboarding and adapter process;
- stable versioned policy and evidence APIs;
- recurring deployment or managed-service demand;
- channel, consortium, university, or platform partnership;
- independent contributors and users;
- recognized use of the programme’s terminology, protocol, benchmark, or conformance format outside the founding repository.

Only at this gate should the programme claim that it is becoming infrastructure or a category.

## 6. Programme metrics

### Evidence metrics

- attributable external sources;
- cases completed;
- blocked, limited, and admitted outcomes;
- source formats and domains;
- independently verified source relationships;
- unresolved semantic fields;
- adapter creation time;
- reproduction time.

### Technical metrics

- benchmark cases;
- invariant and adversarial tests;
- cross-object agreement failures;
- receipt and capsule verification rate;
- release reproducibility;
- external implementations or integrations;
- security and provenance controls.

### Research metrics

- papers submitted, reviewed, revised, accepted, and cited;
- external reviewers;
- methods reused by other researchers;
- datasets or cases used in external work;
- stable archival releases.

### Institutional metrics

- organizations completing a workflow;
- repeat organizational use;
- external operators completing documentation;
- contributors outside the founder;
- partnerships and working groups.

### Commercial metrics

- interviews with identified roles;
- source or design partners;
- letters of interest;
- paid pilots;
- recurring customers;
- revenue, gross margin, onboarding effort, and renewal intent.

### Reputation metrics

- invited talks, demonstrations, reviews, or advisory requests;
- attributable references to the framework;
- independent media or institutional coverage;
- qualified inbound collaborators;
- recognized authorship of the programme’s central concepts.

Repository stars, raw commit count, generated documentation volume, and speculative valuation are secondary diagnostics, not maturity evidence.

## 7. Ownership and institutional structure

### Present structure

The programme remains founder-led research with public open-source components and no claim of a completed company, neutral standard, or independent institution.

### Future separation

Separation should occur only when evidence supports it:

1. **Solarpunk Public Lab** — research, cases, papers, benchmark, open reference implementation, public education, and standards work.
2. **Commercial operating company** — private connectors, institutional deployments, policy packs, hosting, support, and contracts.
3. **Neutral protocol or standard** — specification, conformance suite, governance, and interoperability after independent adoption.

No legal entity should be created merely to make the programme appear larger.

### Ownership controls before commercialization

- dated inventory of pre-existing research, software, data, brands, and documentation;
- explicit public-license inventory;
- contributor agreement or documented contribution policy;
- written treatment of university, grant, employee, contractor, and collaborator rights;
- clear separation of public reference code, private customer data, private connectors, and commercial control-plane work;
- no equity granted only to satisfy programme eligibility.

## 8. Decision and kill rules

### Preserve the research programme when

- the doctrine remains defensible;
- external cases generate useful findings;
- papers, collaborations, scholarships, grants, or institutional work benefit;
- commercial demand remains uncertain or narrow.

### Stop a commercialization hypothesis when

- 20 relevant interviews produce fewer than three serious problem confirmations;
- no qualified source or design partner will provide data or review;
- the deliverable remains fully bespoke with no repeatable workflow;
- potential users admire the project but will not allocate time, authority, or budget;
- legal or institutional constraints eliminate the proposed buyer.

### Build recurring software only when

- three organizations need repeated ingestion or evaluation;
- workflows share a stable core;
- customers request continuing access or monitoring;
- onboarding and support can be standardized;
- security and privacy obligations are supportable.

### Begin category or standard formation only when

- independent organizations use the core concepts or artifacts;
- external contributors exist;
- governance is not controlled solely as a marketing surface;
- conformance has meaning beyond the founding implementation.

## 9. Definition of maximum-value progress

The programme is moving toward maximum value when each new tranche increases at least one of the following without weakening the others:

- external truth;
- reproducibility;
- intellectual distinctiveness;
- institutional dependence;
- ownership-preserving revenue;
- independent participation;
- durable public recognition.

The programme is not moving toward maximum value when work primarily increases internal complexity, visual surface area, speculative scope, or unverifiable claims.
