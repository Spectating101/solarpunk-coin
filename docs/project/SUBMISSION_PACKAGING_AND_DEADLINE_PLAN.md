## 4. FTSID paper identity

### 4.1 Working paper ID

```text
SUB-FTSID-2026-CL-ECI-001
```

### 4.2 Recommended working title

> **From Energy Signals to Monetary Constraints: An Executable Institutional Framework for Evidence-Gated Digital Claims**

Alternative title:

> **Evidence, Authority, Quantity, and Settlement: A Constraint Architecture for Energy-Linked Digital Finance**

The first title better communicates the complete ECI → CL → Policy Lab sequence.

### 4.3 Central paper proposition

> Energy does not discipline a digital financial claim merely because it is physically real or statistically informative. Credible constraint requires purpose-appropriate evidence, authorized action, bounded quantity, explicit uncertainty, defined settlement, anti-reuse, and limited governance. CL–ECI formalizes this sequence, and Policy Lab makes its decisions and failure states executable and reproducible.

### 4.4 Contribution statement

The paper contributes:

1. a sequential distinction between signal, evidence, authority, quantity, settlement, and money;
2. ECI as a purpose-indexed empirical-admissibility layer;
3. Constrained Ledger as a unified institutional constraint architecture;
4. comparative institutional grounding through Norway’s electricity-data, certificate, and flexibility systems;
5. an executable Policy Lab method for blocking, binding-limit attribution, counterfactual replay, settlement stress, and reproducible receipts.

### 4.5 Explicit non-claims

The FTSID paper must state that it does not demonstrate:

- a completed energy-backed currency;
- live Norwegian integration or endorsement;
- legal issuance or settlement authority;
- independent validation of the controlled cases;
- circulation, liquidity, stable value, acceptance, or unit-of-account use;
- production security or deployment readiness.

## 5. Recommended FTSID paper structure

### 1. Introduction

- energy-linked monetary and financial claims often collapse several distinct propositions;
- research question;
- contribution summary;
- explicit scope and non-claims.

### 2. Energy adjacency and the backing fallacy

- energy expenditure, indicators, evidence, backing, and settlement are different;
- explain why physical reality does not automatically create monetary discipline.

### 3. ECI: empirical admissibility

- signal screening dimensions;
- purpose-indexed evidence use;
- include only audited empirical results;
- unresolved or unreproduced results remain excluded or clearly provisional.

### 4. Constrained Ledger: institutional closure

- reliable evidence;
- rule-bound issuance and anti-reuse;
- explicit uncertainty;
- defined settlement and shortfall consequence;
- limited governance.

### 5. Comparative institutional evidence from Norway

- Elhub: measured, estimated, provisional, final, and corrected evidence;
- Guarantees of Origin / NECS: authority, 1-MWh quantity mapping, registry identity, cancellation;
- flexibility admission: registration and prequalification distinct from delivery;
- common institutional pattern;
- limits of the comparison.

Norway should ground the architecture, not dominate the paper. Target approximately 10–15% of the main text.

### 6. Policy Lab as executable research method

- case, evidence, context, policy, decision, settlement, and receipt objects;
- deterministic blocking and binding attribution;
- counterfactual invariance;
- reproducibility and lineage.

### 7. Controlled demonstration

Canonical sequence:

```text
180 units requested
→ L0 assurance
→ blocked by minimum provenance
→ change only declared assurance context to L2
→ evidence identity unchanged
→ 126 units permitted
→ provenance-policy capacity binds
→ 40% settlement stress
→ 50.4 covered / 75.6 shortfall
→ passing issuance did not guarantee settlement
→ monetary performance remains untested
```

The paper must identify the TYN source as a controlled fixture without trusted operator verification or external corroboration.

### 8. Discussion

- why mature institutions separate these functions;
- what the unified architecture adds;
- model risk, revision-aware evidence, and actor separation;
- practical use as a pre-deployment research method.

### 9. Limitations and validation pathway

- empirical audit status;
- controlled evidence;
- no legal effect;
- no monetary-performance evidence;
- next external gate: expert review or one attributable evidence comparison.

### 10. Conclusion

- restate the bounded contribution;
- avoid promotional claims.

## 9. Go/no-go gates

### FTSID — proceed when

- the paper contribution is distinct and stable;
- all central empirical statements have result-ledger status;
- Norway claims map to frozen `NOR-INST-*` entries;
- controlled evidence is labelled honestly;
- figures and artifacts reproduce;
- manuscript is anonymized and formatted;
- the paper can survive without claiming deployment or adoption.

### FTSID — withdraw when

- the central empirical claims remain unreproduced;
- the paper reads mainly as software documentation;
- Norway is used as rhetorical validation rather than bounded evidence;
- the paper depends on implementing `NOR-GO-001` before submission;
- the same argument is being submitted simultaneously in materially overlapping form without venue permission.

### ETHOnline — proceed only when

- the on-chain receipt identity, verification, anti-reuse, or lineage extension is genuinely working;
- the extension is narrow and technically meaningful;
- it does not require a new token, speculative market, or artificial team;
- participation does not endanger the FTSID paper or empirical audit.

Otherwise skip.

### FINEC — proceed only when

- a distinct ECI empirical paper survives result-ledger audit;
- the paper has a result-led rather than architecture-led contribution;
- the £30 fee and later presentation costs are justified.
