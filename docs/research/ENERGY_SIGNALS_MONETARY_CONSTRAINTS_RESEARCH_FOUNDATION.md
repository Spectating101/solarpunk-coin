# 1. Executive synthesis

The research programme can be packaged as one integrated academic argument without collapsing its distinct forms of evidence. Its central problem is the repeated movement from a weak premise — that a variable involves energy — to a strong conclusion — that a monetary or financial claim is credibly constrained by energy. The programme’s contribution is to break that movement into explicit stages, each with its own test and failure mode.

> **ENERGY-ADJACENT SIGNAL**
> ↓ empirical admissibility
> EVIDENTIARY WEIGHT
> ↓ evidence classification and provenance
> PERMITTED SYSTEM ACTIONS
> ↓ admission and quantity rules
> BOUNDED FINANCIAL CLAIM
> ↓ defined settlement and governance
> PERFORMING OR FAILED OBLIGATION
> ↓ only then
> MONETARY TESTING: demand, circulation, liquidity, unit-of-account use

This chain gives each major component a distinct role. ECI evaluates whether an energy-linked signal deserves operational weight. Constrained Ledger specifies what a system may do with evidence at each level of weight. Policy Lab implements those distinctions as deterministic cases, policies, constraints, settlement scenarios, and reproducible receipts. None of the three is sufficient alone, and none should be asked to prove what belongs to another layer.

| Component | Primary question | Academic contribution | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ECI | Does the signal deserve evidentiary weight? | Comparative empirical admissibility standard | Issuance authority, backing, delivery, or money |
| Constrained Ledger | What conditions must bind before evidence supports a claim? | Institutional architecture for evidence, quantity, settlement, and governance | That the chosen data are true, that markets will adopt the claim, or that law will enforce it |
| Policy Lab | Can the distinctions be represented, executed, inspected, and reproduced? | Research artifact and executable decision method | Independent validation, market demand, production security, or legal enforceability |

> **CENTRAL THESIS PROPOSITION**
> Energy does not discipline digital money merely because it is physically real or economically informative. It can constrain a digital financial claim only when an admissible signal is converted into trustworthy, non-reusable evidence and embedded in rules that bind admission, quantity, uncertainty, settlement, and governance.

## What the final thesis should contribute

1. A comparative empirical standard for screening energy-linked indicators before they enter financial design.

1. A falsification of the shortcut from real energy expenditure to monetary backing.

1. A five-condition institutional architecture connecting evidence quality to permitted action.

1. A clear classification of what existing energy-linked instruments prove, transfer, and settle.

1. An explicit treatment of model risk as a quantity and governance problem.

1. An executable research artifact demonstrating admission, binding quantity limits, settlement failure, and reproducible decision identity.

## What it should not claim

- That energy is already money or should replace fiat currency.

- That any macro or sector electricity series authorizes issuance.

- That Bitcoin is backed by electricity because mining consumes energy.

- That satellite or resource-model data prove site-level generation.

- That a signature proves physical truth or ownership.

- That deterministic code proves economic correctness, legal enforceability, or production security.

- That the prototype has demonstrated adoption, liquidity, stable value, convertibility, or crisis performance.

# 3. Core research proposition

## 3.1 The backing fallacy

The programme begins from a recurring analytical error. Energy-linked monetary proposals often collapse four different propositions:

1. A variable has some relation to energy.

1. The variable contains useful information about economic activity or productive capacity.

1. The observation is trustworthy enough to support a financial decision.

1. The observation actually constrains claim creation, quantity, settlement, and governance.

Each proposition has a separate failure mode. A financialized proxy can be energy-adjacent and economically nonspecific. A sector electricity series can be informative but too aggregated to prove site-level production. A revenue-grade meter can prove generation but still say nothing about legal ownership or delivery rights. A flawless evidence object can enter a system whose administrator remains free to ignore the rule. The thesis is valuable because it makes those transitions explicit.

## 3.2 The sequential research question

> Under what empirical and institutional conditions can energy evidence impose a credible constraint on a digital financial claim?

The answer must be sequential:

1. Screen the signal for physical grounding, specificity, temporal validity, timeliness, and decision relevance.

1. Classify the evidence according to what it actually proves.

1. Apply a declared admission policy.

1. Evaluate comparable quantity ceilings and identify the binding constraint.

1. Define the settlement object and shortfall consequence before issuance.

1. Make governance deviation visible and costly.

1. Only after those prerequisites are credible should demand, circulation, liquidity, redemption behaviour, and unit-of-account use be tested.

## 3.3 Subsidiary questions

1. Do financialized crypto measures track real activity robustly, or do they primarily reflect liquidity internal to financial markets?

1. Does aggregate electricity provide timely economic information, and does sector-level electricity improve specificity?

1. Does passive proof-of-work expenditure itself demonstrate monetary discipline?

1. What evidence classes should permit which financial actions?

1. How should uncertainty and model choice affect permitted quantity?

1. What must a holder be owed, and what happens when settlement capacity is insufficient?

1. Can the resulting distinctions be represented in deterministic, inspectable, reproducible software?

## 3.4 The four boundaries

> **SIGNAL ≠ EVIDENCE**
> A useful indicator does not prove that a specific event or asset exists.

> **EVIDENCE ≠ AUTHORITY**
> A trustworthy observation does not itself authorize issuance.

> **AUTHORITY ≠ SETTLEMENT**
> A permitted claim does not establish that the holder can receive what is owed.

> **FINANCIAL CLAIM ≠ MONEY**
> A constrained claim does not become money without circulation, liquidity, acceptance, unit-of-account use, and credible performance under stress.

# 4. Research programme architecture: ECI, CL, and Policy Lab

## 4.1 Layer I — ECI: empirical admissibility

ECI is the empirical gateway. It asks whether an energy-linked variable deserves operational weight and for what decision. It applies a common standard across indirect financialized metrics, broad physical indicators, sector-specific utility data, modelled resource estimates, and eventually site-level observations.

The output of ECI is not a score that mints claims. Its output is a bounded statement about evidentiary relevance: what the signal can inform, how timely and stable it is, which assumptions are material, and what inferences remain prohibited.

## 4.2 Layer II — Constrained Ledger: institutional closure

Constrained Ledger begins where ECI ends. It asks what rules must bind before evidence can support a claim. Its primary contribution is not another token or ledger implementation; it is the specification of jointly necessary conditions:

1. reliable evidence;
2. rule-bound issuance and evidence anti-reuse;
3. explicit treatment of uncertainty;
4. defined settlement and shortfall consequence; and
5. limited governance.

CL translates evidentiary weight into permitted system action. Weak evidence may support context or monitoring. Modelled resource data may support a scenario ceiling. Metered generation may support admission and quantity. Reserved or delivered capacity may support settlement. Downstream engineering cannot upgrade the evidence class that entered.

## 4.3 Layer III — Policy Lab: executable research environment

Policy Lab is the implementation and publication layer. It represents cases, evidence, context, policies, calculators, decision results, settlement results, and receipts as versioned objects. It should allow researchers and reviewers to inspect:

- why a case is blocked;
- which quantity ceiling binds;
- what changes under a declared counterfactual;
- how settlement fails under stress;
- which data, model, policy, calculator, and source revision produced the result.

Policy Lab demonstrates representation and deterministic execution. It does not by itself validate the empirical data, prove physical truth, create legal rights, or establish market demand.

## 4.4 The directional relationship

```text
ECI result
  ↓
evidence role and epistemic weight
  ↓
CL evidence class
  ↓
permitted policy actions
  ↓
Policy Lab case decision
  ↓
receipt and reproduction
```

The direction is one way. Empirical quality bounds permitted action. A smart contract, signature, dashboard, or attractive interface cannot promote a weak signal into stronger evidence.

# 8. ECI: empirical admissibility layer

ECI should be framed as a comparative decision-oriented screen, not a search for one universal “energy coefficient.” The standard matters more than any single preferred candidate.

## 8.1 Admissibility dimensions

| Dimension | Question | Failure mode | Lab representation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Physical grounding | Is the metric tied directly to measured energy production, sale, use, or delivery? | Financial or technological proximity is mistaken for physical evidence. | Source type and evidence/context label |
| Economic specificity | Can movement be connected to a defined activity, sector, facility, or claim? | A broad common cycle is mistaken for a targeted signal. | Domain/sector/site identity |
| Temporal validity | Does the relationship survive stationarity, residual, break, endpoint, and specification checks? | Shared trends or sample choices create apparent information. | Diagnostics and robustness panel |
| Timeliness | Is the observation available before the decision it is supposed to support? | Coincident or lagging description is marketed as forecasting. | Release lag and decision timestamp |
| Operational relevance | What actual decision can the signal support, and what can it not support? | A significant coefficient has no defined system consequence. | Permitted role and prohibited inference |

## 8.2 Output of the ECI layer

The output is an evidence-role recommendation, not issuance quantity.

| Candidate type | Preliminary role | Prohibited interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Financialized crypto proxy | Context, liquidity monitoring, research signal | Real-output evidence or issuance proof |
| Broad physical indicator | Macro/system context | Site, sector, ownership, or delivery proof |
| Sector-specific utility data | Planning or higher-assurance policy context | Facility generation, export, or holder delivery |
| Modelled resource estimate | Scenario or plausibility ceiling with explicit uncertainty | Realised production evidence |
| Metered generation with provenance | Admission and quantity input under policy | Ownership or settlement unless separately established |
| Reserved/delivered capacity | Settlement input | Monetary adoption or universal physical delivery |

## 8.3 Candidate analyses as discriminating cases

Each empirical block should expose a different reason a candidate may fail:

- Crypto measures: indirect mechanism and unstable time-series interpretation.

- Aggregate electricity: physical mechanism but low specificity and limited lead value.

- Sector utility data: improved specificity and timeliness, but still not claim-level evidence.

- Mining expenditure: real cost, but no holder right and a tested ratio vulnerable to persistence.

The chapter should conclude with an ordering of evidentiary roles, not a winner declared as a monetary anchor.

# 9. Constrained Ledger: institutional constraint layer

## 9.1 Five jointly necessary conditions

| Condition | Requirement | Failure without it | Executable representation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reliable evidence | The observation’s source, semantics, time, place, quality, and limitations are explicit. | Weak or false assertions enter with the appearance of precision. | EvidenceEnvelope, provenance/assurance decision, hashes |
| Rule-bound issuance and anti-reuse | A declared policy determines admission and quantity; one evidence event cannot support repeated claims. | Issuance remains discretionary or physically unbounded. | Admission gates, quantity calculators, evidence-consumption registry |
| Explicit uncertainty | Variability and model risk enter price, margin, tolerance, or capacity under visible assumptions. | Uncertainty is hidden, shifted to the holder, or manipulated through model choice. | Context/model manifest, model-dependent quantity ceiling |
| Defined settlement | The holder’s right and the consequence of shortfall are fixed before issuance. | Attribution is marketed as redemption or obligation remains undefined. | Settlement object, capacity, covered/shortfall result |
| Limited governance | Policy changes, overrides, emergency powers, and issuer discretion are visible and costly. | Administrators can waive every other condition when compliance is inconvenient. | Versioned policy, change delay, audit log, authority separation |

No condition substitutes for another. Strong evidence cannot repair undefined settlement. Deterministic settlement cannot repair reused evidence. Governance transparency cannot repair a wrong model, but it can expose model choice and change.

## 9.2 Lifecycle semantics

```text
Evidence admission
  ↓
Policy admission
  ↓
Quantity evaluation
  ↓
Binding constraint attribution
  ↓
Claim creation / issuance
  ↓
Settlement evaluation
  ↓
Settled / partial / shortfall / disputed
```

Admission, quantity, and settlement must remain different object classes and lifecycle stages. Flattening them into one minimum function may look elegant but destroys meaning.

## 9.3 Attribution is not settlement

Energy attribute instruments may establish source, vintage, location, technology, exclusive issuance, transfer, and retirement. They do not give a bearer the physically distinct electricity generated at that event. Power contracts may create delivery or cash obligations, but those rights belong to contractual counterparties and operate through grid, balancing, and settlement systems rather than through universal bearer delivery.

The defensible institutional claim is bounded:

> Within the publicly documented instrument classes included under the stated review rule, source attribution and universal bearer delivery do not co-occur.

The physical reason matters. Grid electricity is commingled. A later settlement promise must therefore name a different object: cash, replacement energy, protected capacity, a certificate retirement, or another pre-defined consequence. Calling attribution “delivery” does not solve the problem.

## 9.4 Evidence hierarchy to permitted action

| Evidence class | Example | Permitted role | Does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Financialized proxy | TVL, stablecoin supply, active addresses | Context/monitoring only | Physical production or backing |
| Broad physical indicator | Aggregate electric-utility output | System context | Sector/site generation or delivery |
| Sector-specific utility data | Semiconductor electricity sales | Planning or policy ceiling input | Facility output or settlement capacity |
| Modelled resource context | NASA POWER/PVWatts | Scenario ceiling and plausibility | Metered production |
| Metered generation | Revenue-grade generation/export data | Admission and quantity input | Ownership or holder right without separate evidence |
| Corroborated/anti-reuse evidence | Meter plus trusted operator/registry | Higher-assurance bounded claim input | Guaranteed future settlement |
| Reserved or delivered capacity | Protected pool, contractual capacity, metered delivery | Settlement ceiling | Money, adoption, or systemic stability |

# 10. Quantity, uncertainty, and model governance

The pricing block should be reconstructed around a clear research contribution: a system is not credibly rule-bound if its quantity ceiling depends on an unstated, inappropriate, or manipulable model.

The inherited GBM analysis should not be defended as a production model. It can remain only as a transparent negative benchmark showing what happens when a bounded, seasonal, mean-reverting physical process is forced into an unbounded diffusion.

## 10.1 Required model sequence

1. Reproduce the exact irradiance source, coordinates, cleaning, smoothing, trimming, and volatility transformation.

1. Resolve every coordinate inconsistency across manuscript, scripts, and state files.

1. Either reproduce the reported volatility exactly or withdraw and regenerate every dependent number.

1. Estimate a seasonal mean-reverting primary model — at minimum an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck residual around deterministic seasonality; preferably compare with a Lucia–Schwartz-style specification where appropriate.

1. Compare GBM, mean-reverting, and sensitivity cases using the same horizon and economic decision.

1. Translate model outputs into declared quantity ceilings, margin, or tolerance rules.

1. Report how often the identity of the binding constraint changes across model choices.

1. Treat material model dependence as a governance result, not a footnote.

## 10.2 Quantity-ceiling architecture

```text
EVIDENCE_BACKED_CAPACITY
PROVENANCE_POLICY_CAPACITY
RESOURCE_CONTEXT_CAPACITY
MODEL_RISK_CAPACITY
LIQUIDITY / MARGIN CAPACITY
SETTLEMENT_RESERVE CAPACITY
ABSOLUTE POLICY CAP
          ↓
minimum comparable applicable ceiling
          ↓
admitted maximum + binding constraint identity
```

Only ceilings with common units and declared conversion semantics may enter the minimum. Missing context should produce explicit blocking, warning, or not-applicable behaviour according to policy; it should not silently become zero or infinity.

The thesis should report both the final maximum and the full ceiling vector. A number without attribution hides the policy mechanism.

# 11. Policy Lab: executable research layer

Policy Lab should be built after the thesis architecture is frozen enough to define stable research objects. Its purpose is not to decorate the thesis but to expose the argument for investigation.

## 11.1 Minimum research functions

- Open a declared case and inspect evidence, context, policy, and identities.

- State the current answer first: blocked, admitted with limit, or settlement outcome.

- Show each admission rule and blocking reason.

- Show every applicable quantity ceiling in a common unit and identify the binding rule or tie set.

- Fork explicit counterfactuals without mutating the original case or evidence identity.

- Compare policies on one case and cases under one policy.

- Replay settlement and adversarial stress.

- Inspect lineage from source and transform to decision and claim.

- Export machine-readable and human-readable decision receipts.

- Link every thesis claim to a result, rule, case, receipt, limitation, or reproduction object.

## 11.2 Five-minute investigation

The flagship demonstration should fit one coherent flow:

1. Open a Taoyuan energy case under a pilot policy.

1. Observe BLOCKED because minimum provenance is not met; quantity is not evaluated.

1. Inspect exactly which evidence property is missing.

1. Create an explicit assurance counterfactual while keeping the original evidence hash unchanged.

1. Observe ADMIT WITH LIMIT and the full vector of quantity ceilings.

1. Identify the binding ceiling and compare another case under the same policy.

1. Apply settlement capacity at full, partial, and zero levels.

1. Export the decision and comparison receipts.

The counterfactual must be labelled as a declared scenario, not an actual upgrade to evidence.

# 16. Boundaries, anti-patterns, and claims to avoid

## 16.1 Core claim boundaries

| May claim after completion | Must not claim without later evidence |
| --- | --- |
| The programme provides an empirical admissibility screen for energy-linked signals. | That the screen identifies one universal energy anchor. |
| The tested cumulative-cost shortcut fails the stated falsification tests if reproduced. | That no mining-cost or marginal-cost relation can ever exist. |
| Five conditions can be specified as necessary for a credible energy-linked financial constraint. | That they are sufficient, optimal, legally enforceable, or complete. |
| The reviewed public instrument classes separate attribution from universal bearer delivery. | That no bespoke contract anywhere can combine related features. |
| Model choice materially affects quantity and should be governed explicitly. | That one final pricing model is universally correct. |
| Policy Lab represents and executes declared rules under controlled conditions. | That it proves physical truth, oracle robustness, production security, or independent validation. |
| A public-network transaction occurred under the frozen implementation. | That public execution proves reserves, redemption, market value, or law. |
| The artifact is a constrained financial-claim framework. | That it is demonstrated money, a stablecoin, or a deployable monetary system. |

## 16.2 Anti-patterns

- Narrative laundering. Treating a generated sentence as support for a result.

- Coefficient worship. Allowing significance to override mechanism, stationarity, timing, or specificity.

- Physicality laundering. Treating anything involving electricity as direct energy evidence.

- Signature laundering. Treating cryptographic validity as physical truth, ownership, or trustworthy operator context.

- Token laundering. Treating issuance on a blockchain as backing or settlement.

- Receipt laundering. Treating attribution or lineage as delivery.

- Model laundering. Hiding policy choice inside a technical parameter.

- Counterfactual laundering. Presenting a hypothetical assurance upgrade as real evidence.

- Blockchain laundering. Treating public execution as proof of sound economics, secure oracles, legal rights, or reserves.

- Interface inflation. Adding maps, wallets, AI chat, dashboards, and market features faster than the research can support them.

- Scope substitution. Turning a master’s thesis about prerequisites into a claim to have solved currency design, monetary policy, or global energy settlement.

- Defensive repetition. Repeating caveats in every paragraph instead of designing clear claim boundaries once and writing confidently inside them.

## 16.3 Product features to defer

- Mainnet or production deployment

- Customer authentication and multi-tenant SaaS

- Marketplace, exchange, or generalized issuance platform

- DAO governance and token-economic expansion

- AI decision authority

- Live global GIS as the primary interface

- Claims of legal approval, reserve custody, redemption, or settlement guarantee

- Monetary adoption experiments before evidence, quantity, and settlement prerequisites are credible

# 18. Final working doctrine

The programme should be rebuilt around a simple hierarchy of authority. The underlying data and documents outrank old prose. Reproducible results outrank remembered outputs. The claim registry outranks chapter narrative. The thesis outranks the interface. The frozen decision engine outranks screenshots. External evidence outranks controlled realism. Monetary functions remain a later research stage.

> **THE COMPLETE DOCTRINE**
> Screen the signal. Classify the evidence. Declare the policy. Separate admission, quantity, and settlement. Expose model risk. Prevent evidence reuse. Make governance deviation visible. Preserve the receipt. Test monetary functions only after these prerequisites hold.

This packaging allows the full depth of the project to remain visible without asking one document to prove everything. ECI supplies the empirical gateway. Constrained Ledger supplies the institutional architecture. Policy Lab supplies the executable research environment. The thesis explains why the three must be connected and why none is sufficient alone. The replication package makes the claims auditable. The later lab turns the finished research into a public, inspectable, versioned programme rather than another broad SolarPunk showcase.

The immediate objective is therefore not to polish the existing 55-page draft. It is to complete the result ledger, freeze the contribution architecture in this foundation document, and reconstruct the thesis from verified research objects. Once that is done, the interface can be built with confidence because it will publish a stable academic argument rather than trying to discover one through design.
