# Fiscal Choke Points — Hostile Publication Audit

**Manuscript:** `FISCAL_CHOKEPOINTS_PUBLICATION_CANDIDATE_2026.md`  
**Audit posture:** assume a skeptical public-finance/tax-administration reviewer is looking for a reason to reject.  
**Date:** 2026-09-14

## Executive verdict

**Internal verdict: PASS AS A VENUE-NEUTRAL PUBLICATION CANDIDATE, WITH EXTERNAL/PROCESS GATES.**

The paper now has a defensible contribution only because it has been narrowed against prior art. It must not sell `fiscal choke point`, platform functional capability, payment control, or taxing-point timing as newly discovered concepts. OECD and broader tax-administration literature already contain those ideas.

The publishable contribution is the **source-controlled seven-path comparative representation**, the separation of `node locus` and `event coupling` as explicit cross-instrument coding dimensions, the use of internal countercases to test node activation, and the Stage-A/Stage-B separation between legal architecture and downstream procedural evidence.

The major substantive limitation is deliberate: the paper does not identify which design performs better. That makes venue fit important. A reviewer expecting causal tax-performance estimation may reject the paper as descriptive; a reviewer interested in comparative tax administration, digital regulation, or institutional design should see a coherent contribution.

## 1. Novelty challenge: “choke points” are not new

### Reviewer attack

> “Taxing at concentrated points in a supply chain is old. Platform liability is old. Why is this new?”

### Assessment

**VALID ATTACK AGAINST AN OVERBROAD VERSION OF THE PAPER.**

The package now concedes:

- firms as fiscal intermediaries;
- VAT information trails;
- platform liability;
- function/capability criteria;
- payment and taxing-point considerations;
- concentrated/choke-point supply-chain taxation;
- regulatory intermediation;
- ASEAN digital-tax comparison.

The term **fiscal choke point** is therefore an organizing label, not the originality claim.

### Durable novelty

The paper adds:

- path-level decomposition rather than country-level tax labels;
- seven coded paths including multiple Philippine nodes;
- node locus/event coupling as explicit independent comparison dimensions;
- within-regime countercases;
- Stage A legal architecture / Stage B procedural-capacity separation;
- source-controlled inference limits preventing performance ranking.

### Verdict

**PASS after explicit prior-art concession.**

## 2. “This is still just a descriptive legal comparison”

### Reviewer attack

> “You have read five countries' rules and invented a taxonomy. There is no identification.”

### Assessment

**MAIN VENUE-FIT RISK.**

The paper should not pretend otherwise. It is a comparative institutional study, not a causal econometric paper.

What moves it beyond a simple country survey is:

1. the path rather than country as the unit;
2. reproducible coding rules;
3. multiple nodes inside one jurisdiction;
4. internal countercases where broad platform classifications fail;
5. rival-explanation controls;
6. a second operational-capacity evidence layer;
7. explicit falsification/extension requirements.

This is sufficient for an institutional/tax-administration contribution, but not for a venue whose threshold is causal policy-effect estimation.

### Verdict

**PASS FOR THE RIGHT VENUE; POTENTIAL DESK-REJECT AT EMPIRICS-FIRST VENUES.**

## 3. Comparability of unlike tax instruments

### Reviewer attack

> “Malaysia SToDS, Indonesia PMSE VAT, Vietnam seller withholding, Thai e-service VAT, and Philippine paths are legally different. Why compare them?”

### Assessment

This is a legitimate design question, and the manuscript has the correct answer: it does **not** claim the taxes are equivalent. It compares one deliberately narrow institutional layer—where responsibility is located and coupled to the transaction.

The paper must keep tax bases, rates, and revenue totals out of the common comparative outcome space.

### Failure condition

Any cross-country regression, ranking, or revenue comparison that treats these paths as equivalent without a new harmonization design would invalidate the current method.

### Verdict

**PASS UNDER THE NARROW ADMINISTRATIVE COMPARISON.**

---

## 4. Operational-control interpretation

### Reviewer attack

> “You say duties follow operational control. But legal doctrine, OECD diffusion, political feasibility, and administrative capacity may explain node choice instead.”

### Assessment

Correct. The paper's data support operational functions as repeated **activation conditions / correlates**, not as the sole causal determinant of legislative choice.

The current manuscript and robustness package preserve rival explanations.

### Required wording

Prefer:

> “responsibility repeatedly attaches to actors performing administratively relevant control functions in the selected paths”

rather than:

> “governments choose these actors because they control the transaction.”

### Verdict

**PASS IF CAUSAL SELECTION LANGUAGE REMAINS BOUNDED.**

---

## 5. Boundary-case falsifiability

### Reviewer attack

> “Your taxonomy merely describes positive cases and can fit anything after the fact.”

### Assessment

This was a serious weakness earlier. It is now materially improved.

The current package includes source-controlled countercases:

- Thai platform presence without the continuous-process condition;
- Philippine marketplace presence with direct payment outside marketplace control;
- Philippine B2B buyer node;
- Vietnamese platform pathways differentiated by order/payment functionality;
- Indonesian commercial presence without appointed-collector status.

These do not prove the framework universally, but they establish that the coding has observable boundaries rather than simply labeling every intermediary a choke point.

### Verdict

**STRONG PASS RELATIVE TO THE EARLIER VERSION.**

## 6. Node locus / event coupling novelty

### Reviewer attack

> “Who is liable and when tax is due are ordinary tax-law concepts. Renaming them does not create novelty.”

### Assessment

Correct. The underlying concepts are prior art.

The paper's contribution is the **explicit two-dimensional comparative coding and application across heterogeneous paths**, not invention of liable actor or taxing point.

The manuscript must retain that distinction, especially in the abstract and cover letter.

### Verdict

**PASS AS A COMPARATIVE REPRESENTATION, NOT A NEW FUNDAMENTAL TAX THEORY.**

## 7. Stage-B operational-capacity layer

### Reviewer attack

> “Countries with better English-language/public documentation will look more administratively capable. You are measuring documentation, not capacity.”

### Assessment

**VALID AND IMPORTANT.**

The manuscript explicitly states that procedural documentation depth is not performance. Thailand's detailed VES guidance cannot be used to rank it above other countries.

The Stage-B matrix records **evidence of mechanisms**, not their effectiveness.

### Failure condition

Reject the comparison if later text creates a maturity score or interprets `EVIDENCED` as high performance and `UNRESOLVED` as failure.

### Verdict

**PASS UNDER CURRENT NON-RANKING RULE.**

## 8. Administrative collection figures

### Reviewer attack

> “Indonesia's Rp36.69tn collection figure is endogenous to market size, tax base, time, enforcement, and many other factors. Why include it?”

### Assessment

It is useful only as an **administrative fact establishing real operation/scale**, not as an outcome comparison or treatment effect.

The claim register and manuscript enforce that boundary.

### Verdict

**PASS AS CONTEXT; NOT AN EFFECT ESTIMATE.**

---

## 9. Why not estimate outcomes now?

### Reviewer attack

> “If the architecture matters, show that it changes compliance or revenue.”

### Assessment

This is the obvious next paper, but forcing it into the current evidence would recreate the methodological failure of the old Digital Tax project.

Comparable matching, audit yield, errors, refunds, compliance costs, seller coverage, and behavioral outcomes are not currently harmonized. The correct research response is to define the architecture first and specify future identified designs—appointment waves, node shifts, threshold reforms, or transaction-level withholding transitions.

### Verdict

**PASS AS A SCOPE DECISION. The absence of causal outcomes is a limitation, not a hidden failure.**

## 10. Governance and normative balance

### Assessment

The publication candidate now includes these costs and explicitly refuses a universal pro-intermediation prescription.

The policy question is framed as which actor should perform which bounded function under what accountability constraints.

### Verdict

**PASS.**
