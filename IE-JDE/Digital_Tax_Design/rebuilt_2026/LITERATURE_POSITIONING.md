# Digital Tax 2026 — Literature Positioning

This note fixes the novelty claim for the rebuilt *Fiscal Choke Points* paper. It is a research-control document: it states what the manuscript must concede as prior art, what it may synthesize, and what remains a defensible contribution.

## 1. What is already established

### A. Firms as fiscal intermediaries

Kleven, Kreiner, and Saez (2016) explain how firms with accurate internal records can become effective third-party reporters and thereby relax tax-enforcement constraints.

**Implication:** assigning fiscal duties to information-rich private actors is not novel.

### B. VAT paper trails and third-party information

Pomeranz (2015) provides experimental evidence that VAT paper trails can create deterrence and enforcement spillovers.

**Implication:** transaction records and reconciliation are established sources of fiscal capacity.

### C. Regulatory intermediation is broader prior art

Abbott, Levi-Faur, and Snidal (2017) formalize the regulator–intermediary–target (R–I–T) model. Regulation can operate through an intermediary that stands between a regulator and the ultimate target and performs implementation, monitoring, certification, information, or enforcement functions.

**Implication:** *Fiscal Choke Points* must not claim to invent the idea that public authority can govern through private intermediaries. Its contribution is a narrower tax-administration specialization: identifying **which transaction node is legally activated, for what bounded fiscal duty, through what operational rail, and how tightly that duty is coupled to the commercial event**.

### D. Platform VAT/GST liability

OECD (2019) explicitly analyzes regimes in which digital platforms can be made fully or partially responsible for assessing, collecting, and remitting VAT/GST on online sales. Administrative concentration is a known policy rationale.

**Implication:** platform collection/liability is direct prior art. `Fiscal choke point` is not a newly invented tax instrument.

### E. Administration of imported digital services and low-value goods

Brondolo (2021) and the OECD/WBG/ADB Asia-Pacific VAT Digital Toolkit (2022) address registration, simplified compliance, vendor collection, platform roles, and implementation design.

**Implication:** the paper belongs inside tax-administration scholarship, not outside it as a discovery of digital taxation.

### F. Platforms as an administrative opportunity

Aslam and Shah (2017) note that peer-to-peer platforms can intensify small-business tax problems while simultaneously creating an administrative opportunity because platforms record transactions and participant activity.

**Implication:** the link between platform data and fragmented-taxpayer administration is established.

### G. Platform governance and infrastructural power are adjacent literatures

Platform-governance research studies how platforms control participation, information, ranking, quality, and market rules. Work on digital platforms and state authority also considers how platform infrastructure can reshape public capacity and dependency.

**Implication:** these literatures can deepen the discussion of why operational control matters and why delegation can create governance costs. They are **adjacent interpretation**, not evidence that a particular tax rule is effective and not the primary novelty claim.

### H. ASEAN digital-tax comparison is prior art

The Philippines National Tax Research Center has compared VAT and VAT-like taxation of digital transactions across ASEAN, emphasizing variation in rates, structures, and collection methods. Comparative legal scholarship has also examined Indonesia and other ASEAN digital-tax regimes.

**Implication:** do not claim the first ASEAN digital-tax comparison.

## 2. The paper's defensible contribution

The defensible contribution is a **transaction-path architecture for comparing fiscal responsibility inside digital commerce**.

### Contribution 1 — a different unit of comparison

Existing comparisons commonly organize regimes by tax type, rate, threshold, nexus rule, or jurisdiction. *Fiscal Choke Points* instead isolates:

```text
commercial event
→ legally covered object
→ liable / reporting node
→ destination or nexus evidence
→ transaction / withholding / filing rail
→ reconciliation relationship
→ bounded fiscal action
```

The term **fiscal choke point** names the legally activated connection inside this sequence. It is a comparative construct, not a claim that regulatory intermediation, platform liability, or ASEAN comparison is new.

### Contribution 2 — five components

The paper operationalizes each path through:

1. taxable object;
2. liable node;
3. destination/nexus evidence;
4. transaction rail;
5. reconciliation power.

This permits a bounded comparison without pooling unlike tax bases, revenue measures, or legal duties.

### Contribution 3 — node locus and event coupling

The paper separates two dimensions that are often buried inside tax-type labels:

- **node locus:** which actor is legally activated;
- **event coupling:** how closely the fiscal duty is tied to transaction/payment/confirmation rather than later periodic administration.

The research package now codes these at the **transaction-path level**, not only the country level. This matters because one jurisdiction can deliberately use several nodes for different transaction classes.

### Contribution 4 — explicit separation of architecture from performance

The package distinguishes:

- legal architecture;
- operational administrative facts;
- reconciliation/matching evidence;
- causal performance evidence.

A legal choke point is therefore not automatically evidence of high compliance, low cost, superior welfare, or effective enforcement.

## 3. Novelty sentence to defend

Use wording in this territory:

> This paper does not introduce regulatory intermediation, platform tax collection, or ASEAN digital-tax comparison as new. It contributes a transaction-path framework for identifying which commercial node is legally activated for a bounded fiscal duty, through which rail, and with what degree of event coupling, and applies that framework consistently across five Southeast Asian jurisdictions.

Avoid:

> Governments are newly discovering how to use platforms as tax collectors.

> Fiscal choke points are a new form of taxation invented in Southeast Asia.

> This is the first comparative study of digital taxation in ASEAN.

> Platform intermediation itself is the paper's novel theory.

## 4. Comparative propositions

`COMPARATIVE_PROPOSITIONS.md` controls the analytical claims developed from the cases. The current propositions are deliberately falsifiable and bounded:

1. fiscal responsibility tends to follow operational control functions;
2. event coupling is a distinct design dimension;
3. node locus and event coupling are not the same variable;
4. destination/nexus evidence solves a different administrative problem from collection responsibility;
5. concentrated intermediation can reduce administrative distance without creating complete visibility;
6. formal law and operational fiscal capacity are separate stages;
7. tax-type labels underdescribe the administrative path;
8. within-country and over-time node changes are the strongest route to later identified effects.

These are not a maturity ranking.

## 5. Rival explanations the paper must preserve

The framework should survive serious alternatives rather than dismiss them:

- node choice may be largely determined by tax type;
- OECD/international template diffusion may explain common architecture;
- administrative digital maturity may constrain feasible node choices;
- platform market concentration may determine whether intermediation is administratively attractive;
- formal law may overstate actual filing, matching, audit, or enforcement capacity;
- the five-case sample is selected partly on observability and source access;
- delegation to platforms can create compliance, privacy, competition, and due-process costs.

`ROBUSTNESS_AND_RIVAL_EXPLANATIONS.md` specifies the associated tests.

## 6. What the coded evidence currently supports

`COUNTRY_PATH_CODINGS.csv` and its deterministic derivative show that:

- the five-country set contains multiple liable-node classes rather than one generic platform node;
- event-coupling classes vary;
- the Philippines requires separate B2C, B2B, and marketplace paths, showing that country is sometimes too coarse a coding unit;
- node locus and event coupling can be stored and reviewed separately;
- the coded paths span more than one legal object, so only the administrative node layer is compared across the full set.

These are descriptive architecture findings, not performance results.

## 7. Literature-facing research gap

The gap should be framed as:

1. public-finance theory explains why third-party records and fiscal intermediaries matter;
2. regulatory-intermediary theory explains governance through private intermediaries;
3. VAT/platform guidance explains platform/vendor collection and implementation;
4. ASEAN comparative work maps rates, structures, nexus, and collection methods;
5. platform-governance scholarship explains why private operational control can be consequential;
6. **the remaining contribution is a source-controlled transaction-path architecture that makes the locus and timing of fiscal responsibility directly comparable while preserving tax-object heterogeneity and separating formal architecture from operational performance.**

This is narrower than a new general theory of digital taxation. It is also more defensible.

## 8. Required literature set

At minimum:

- Kleven, Kreiner & Saez (2016) — firms as fiscal intermediaries;
- Pomeranz (2015) — VAT information trails;
- Abbott, Levi-Faur & Snidal (2017) — regulatory intermediaries / R–I–T model;
- OECD (2019) — digital-platform VAT/GST liability;
- Brondolo (2021) — imported digital-services/LVG administration;
- OECD/WBG/ADB (2022) — Asia-Pacific VAT digital toolkit;
- Aslam & Shah (2017) — platform technology and P2P tax administration;
- Keen & Slemrod (2017) — tax-administration design;
- at least one prior ASEAN-wide government comparison;
- at least one prior ASEAN comparative legal study.

Platform-governance and infrastructural-power work can be used as an adjacent discussion layer where it clarifies control and delegation costs, but it should not displace the tax-administration literature.

## 9. Hostile-review test

A skeptical reviewer should be able to say:

> I already knew firms and platforms could act as regulatory or tax intermediaries, VAT information trails matter, and ASEAN digital-tax systems have been compared. What this paper adds is a disciplined transaction-path method for showing which node is legally activated, through which rail, how tightly the duty is coupled to the commercial event, and where the evidence stops before operational performance.

If the paper cannot elicit that reaction, narrow the novelty claim again.
