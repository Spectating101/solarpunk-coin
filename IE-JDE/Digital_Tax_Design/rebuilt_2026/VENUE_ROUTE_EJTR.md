# Fiscal Choke Points — Primary Venue Route

## Primary route: eJournal of Tax Research (UNSW)

**Route status:** best current fit among checked tax journals; verify rules again immediately before submission.

## Why this route

The eJournal of Tax Research is peer reviewed and explicitly interdisciplinary. Its current submission page welcomes original work on a broad range of tax topics and addresses academics, practitioners, and policymakers. Its recent issues include tax administration, third-party appointments, compliance, technology, and comparative/institutional work.

That fits the actual paper better than an empirics-first public-finance route.

The main editorial risk is explicit: the journal says articles will not normally be published if they are **purely descriptive**. The submission must therefore foreground the parts that move Fiscal Choke Points beyond a country survey:

- seven transaction paths rather than five country summaries;
- reproducible coding dimensions;
- within-regime boundary/countercases;
- Stage-A / Stage-B separation;
- falsifiable extension requirements;
- no unsupported country ranking.

## Route-specific abstract — 95 words

> Digital tax systems do more than set rates: they assign fiscal duties to actors inside transaction paths. Using primary legal and administrative sources from Malaysia, Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand, and the Philippines, this paper codes seven paths by taxable object, liable node, nexus evidence, transaction rail, reconciliation power, node locus, and event coupling. Within-regime countercases show that platform presence alone is insufficient for liability. A second layer separates legal assignment from filing, correction, refund, audit, appeal, enforcement, and outcomes. The framework enables comparative tax-administration analysis without pooling unlike tax bases or inferring performance from procedural documentation.

## Route-specific cover-letter emphasis

The cover letter should lead with **comparative tax administration**, not “digital tax rates.”

Recommended core:

> The manuscript is not a descriptive survey of five national tax systems. It decomposes the systems into seven legally distinct transaction paths, applies a reproducible architecture, and uses within-regime countercases to test when intermediary presence is insufficient to activate a fiscal duty. A second evidence layer distinguishes legal node assignment from filing, correction, refund, audit, appeal, enforcement and observed outcomes. The paper deliberately does not pool unlike tax bases or infer administrative performance from documentation density.
