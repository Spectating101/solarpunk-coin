# Digital Tax 2026 — Claim Boundaries

This file defines the inference limits for the rebuilt Digital Tax project. It exists to prevent historical claims from re-entering the current manuscript merely because they remain elsewhere in the repository.

## 1. Platform accounting is not a tax base

The project may compare platform revenue with platform-mediated transaction measures for accounting or institutional analysis. It may **not** infer that the difference is automatically:

- hidden GDP;
- participant income;
- taxable income;
- unpaid tax;
- tax evasion;
- an administratively recoverable revenue pool.

Any transition from a commercial transaction measure to a legal tax base requires an explicit taxable object, jurisdictional rule, liable person or entity, period, exemptions or thresholds, and applicable administrative mechanism.

## 2. Revenue growth is not causal identification

Administrative revenue series can establish that collections were reported and can document scale or growth. They do not, by themselves, identify why revenue changed.

Do not infer a causal effect from before/after revenue movements without a design that can distinguish the intervention from market growth, rate changes, coverage changes, exchange-rate movements, enforcement changes, registration expansion, timing effects, or other contemporaneous factors.

The inherited Malaysia low-value-goods DiD is not canonical evidence in the rebuild unless it is independently reconstructed and survives a fresh identification audit.

## 3. Cross-country heterogeneity must remain visible

Malaysia, Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand, and the Philippines do not implement one identical digital tax instrument.

Do not pool or rank jurisdictions as though the following were interchangeable without an explicit harmonization argument:

- digital-services VAT/GST/service-tax collection;
- low-value-goods import tax;
- platform withholding for sellers;
- foreign-supplier registration;
- domestic-buyer reverse charge or withholding;
- gross collection figures reported in different currencies and periods.

Comparative analysis should focus first on institutional architecture: taxable object, liable node, destination evidence, transaction rail, and reconciliation power.

## 4. Fiscal choke point is a comparative construct

`Fiscal choke point` is an author-defined analytical term for a legally enforceable transaction node where a state can assign bounded fiscal duties to an actor controlling relevant commercial records, payment, access, or transaction execution.

The project must not imply that platform tax intermediation, third-party reporting, VAT collection by platforms, withholding, or destination-based consumption taxation were invented by this project.

The contribution to defend is narrower and stronger:

> heterogeneous ASEAN digital-tax regimes can be compared as different architectures for activating fiscal responsibility inside private transaction infrastructure.

Literature positioning must distinguish this synthesis from established work on third-party reporting, firms as fiscal intermediaries, VAT/GST platform liability, and tax administration.

## 5. Legal dates require event typing

For every regime change, distinguish where possible:

- announcement date;
- enactment or issuance date;
- statutory effective date;
- operational start date;
- first reporting or collection period.

Do not collapse these into a single “began” date when the sources distinguish them. Where sources conflict or implementation changed after announcement, the claim remains unresolved until the operative legal source is frozen.

## 6. Administrative capacity must be measured directly where possible

Collection totals are an outcome, not a complete measure of fiscal capacity.

Prefer additional administrative indicators where available:

- number of appointed or registered remitters;
- filing rates;
- declared taxable sales;
- payment timeliness;
- matched or reconciled records;
- refund activity;
- audit or enforcement coverage;
- compliance cost or onboarding burden.

If these are unavailable, state the limitation rather than treating revenue as a sufficient proxy.

## 7. Comparative inference cannot exceed country evidence

A regional claim is valid only when it can be reconstructed from supported jurisdiction-level claims.

Allowed pattern:

```text
country claim + country claim + country claim
→ explicit comparison rule
→ bounded regional inference
```

Disallowed pattern:

```text
regional narrative
→ retroactive selection of country anecdotes
```

## 8. Current prohibited canonical claims

The following remain historical hypotheses or superseded findings unless independently re-established:

- “digital-tax rates do not matter”;
- “base breadth dominates rates by 30:1”;
- “compliance fully mediates the rate effect”;
- “Malaysia LVG proves a +$114M causal effect”;
- “destination-basis taxation eliminates tax competition”;
- “the ASEAN panel proves no strategic rate interaction”;
- publication-readiness or acceptance-probability percentages from the inherited package.

## 9. Relationship to Invisible Ledger

Invisible Ledger and Digital Tax are complementary but distinct.

Invisible Ledger asks what different accounting and transaction measures actually represent. Digital Tax asks how law and administration attach fiscal duties to observable transactions.

The Digital Tax paper should therefore cite Invisible Ledger as a measurement companion, not as proof of hidden tax liability.

## 10. Hostile-review rule

When two phrasings are possible, use the strongest wording supported by the frozen evidence — not the strongest wording that would make the paper sound more important.

A claim should be weakened, qualified, or removed whenever the source establishes less than the manuscript sentence implies.
