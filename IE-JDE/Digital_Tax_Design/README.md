# Digital Tax Design — 2026 Rebuild

**Status:** active research rebuild  
**Canonical direction:** fiscal choke points / platform fiscal intermediation  
**Working branch:** `digital-tax/rebuild-2026`

## Current research question

> How do governments convert control over digital transactions into administrable fiscal capacity?

The 2026 rebuild treats digital taxation primarily as an institutional-design problem: governments assign reporting, identification, collection, withholding, and remittance duties to actors that already control commercially useful transaction records or payment flows.

The core analytical object is the **fiscal choke point**: a legally activated transaction node where a state can attach a bounded fiscal obligation to an actor capable of observing or controlling the relevant commercial event.

## Canonical components

A fiscal choke point is analyzed through five components:

1. **Taxable object** — the sale, service, import, payment, or facilitated transaction that enters the legal base.
2. **Liable node** — the supplier, platform, payment intermediary, importer, or buyer assigned the duty.
3. **Destination evidence** — the rule and evidence used to locate the customer or consumption.
4. **Transaction rail** — the checkout, payment, invoicing, filing, or withholding process through which the duty becomes operational.
5. **Reconciliation power** — the lawful ability to compare declarations with transaction records and audit compliance.

## Relationship to Invisible Ledger

The rebuild does **not** treat platform gross transaction value, platform-mediated commerce, participant income, GDP, or taxable income as interchangeable quantities.

`Invisible Ledger` studies the measurement and accounting boundary between platform financial statements and the larger transaction systems platforms coordinate. Digital Tax starts from that distinction and asks a different question: where can fiscal responsibility legally and administratively attach inside those transaction systems?

The relationship is therefore:

```text
Invisible Ledger
measurement / accounting boundary
        ↓
Digital Tax
administrative / institutional response
```

Neither project should describe the platform-revenue / transaction-value difference as automatically hidden GDP, unpaid tax, tax evasion, or a directly taxable base.

## Research authority

The current rebuild lives under [`rebuilt_2026/`](./rebuilt_2026/).

Files elsewhere in `IE-JDE/Digital_Tax_Design/` document the **pre-rebuild Digital Tax programme**. They remain useful as research history and for tracing why the project changed direction, but they are not current evidentiary authority unless a claim is independently revalidated in the rebuild.

In particular, the following inherited claims are **superseded as canonical findings** and must not be presented as established results without a fresh design and validation:

- statutory digital-tax rates do not predict revenue;
- base breadth produces a 30:1 revenue advantage over rate changes;
- compliance fully mediates the effect of statutory rates;
- the Malaysia low-value-goods change establishes a causal revenue effect through the inherited DiD design;
- destination-basis taxation eliminates digital tax competition;
- the inherited 17-observation panel is sufficient to establish cross-country causal mechanisms;
- publication probability or journal-readiness percentages stated in older package documents.

See [`rebuilt_2026/CLAIM_BOUNDARIES.md`](./rebuilt_2026/CLAIM_BOUNDARIES.md) for the current claim discipline.

## What the rebuild may claim

The rebuild may make comparative-institutional claims when supported by primary legal or administrative sources. Examples include:

- a jurisdiction assigns collection or reporting responsibility to specified digital suppliers or platforms;
- a regime uses defined evidence to determine customer or consumption location;
- a platform or buyer becomes the legally designated remitting node for a defined transaction class;
- administrative records show a named population of registered or appointed remitters and reported collections;
- different ASEAN jurisdictions implement distinct transaction-level routes into platform commerce.

Revenue growth by itself does **not** identify why collection changed. It can reflect market growth, tax-rate changes, coverage changes, registration expansion, enforcement, timing, or other factors. Causal language requires a design capable of supporting it.

## Rebuild acceptance standard

Digital Tax v2 is not considered research-complete merely because the narrative paper reads well. The package should close the following gates:

- every central country claim maps to an inspectable source;
- unlike legal instruments and revenue bases are not pooled into a misleading regional total;
- platform transaction value is never substituted for a legal tax base;
- comparative categories are defined consistently across countries;
- current and superseded claims are clearly separated;
- the paper, source catalog, structured country table, and validation notes agree;
- limitations are explicit where source comparability or causal identification is weak;
- the final manuscript can survive an independent claim-by-claim hostile audit.

## Historical files

The existing manuscript templates, tax-competition model, DiD analysis, mechanism analysis, robustness files, and older submission materials are preserved as historical work. Their presence in the repository does not grant them current authority.

Do not follow older instructions that say to integrate those files unchanged, avoid questioning the research design, or treat their statistics as already validated. The 2026 rebuild exists specifically because that evidentiary posture was too strong for the underlying design.
