# Invisible Ledger — SSRN Supersession Note

## Why this is required

A public SSRN working paper under the Invisible Ledger name remains indexed with the earlier residual-centered argument. Its abstract reports an ASEAN-wide “unmeasured platform economy,” an Indonesia residual/wedge, a 12.x ecosystem ratio, and Malaysia policy evidence used as validation of that interpretation.

Those claims are **not** the governing claims of the 2026 publication candidate.

A journal editor or reviewer searching the title/author can encounter the old preprint. Leaving it unexplained creates three risks:

1. reviewers may evaluate the new paper against claims it explicitly abandoned;
2. they may interpret the new manuscript as an undisclosed duplicate rather than a fundamental revision;
3. stale quantitative claims may continue to circulate after the research design has rejected them.

## Governing publication-candidate position

The new paper does not retain:

- an ASEAN `$185B/$192B` hidden/unmeasured-economy aggregate;
- a 12.x ecosystem/fiscal multiplier as an economic statistic;
- `transaction value - platform revenue` as missing GDP, participant income, taxable income, or tax gap;
- Malaysia LVG as causal validation of that residual;
- calibrated ASEAN take rates as population parameters.

The new paper studies cross-record reconciliation in Indonesia and treats the old residual framework as superseded.

## Recommended public working-paper action

Before or alongside journal submission, replace the SSRN manuscript with the new working-paper version where SSRN's revision controls permit it, retaining the historical version record rather than pretending it never existed.

The revised SSRN abstract should match the publication candidate's bounded contribution.

## Suggested version note

> **Major revision / supersession notice (September 2026).** This version fundamentally revises the project's research question, empirical design, and claims. Earlier versions interpreted the difference between platform-mediated transaction value and platform revenue as an “unmeasured” or “invisible” economy and extended that residual across ASEAN. That interpretation is no longer retained. The revised paper treats transaction value, platform revenue, national e-commerce statistics, payment-system records, income, value added, and taxable bases as distinct economic objects. It focuses on Indonesia and studies how digital-commercial growth changes interpretation across issuer accounting, national statistics, payment traces, and administrative reporting architecture. Prior ASEAN residual estimates, multiplier language, and the earlier Malaysia validation claim should not be cited as findings of the current paper.

## Suggested revised SSRN title

**The Invisible Ledger: Reconciling Digital Commerce Across Corporate, Statistical, Payment, and Administrative Records in Indonesia**

## Suggested revised SSRN abstract

Digital commerce is recorded through platform operating metrics and revenue, national e-commerce statistics, payment-system activity, and administrative records. These quantities are related but do not measure the same economic object. This paper provides a source-auditable reconciliation of those records in Indonesia. Audited GoTo disclosures show Tokopedia e-commerce GTV falling 8.90% from FY2022 to FY2023 while third-party net segment revenue rises 53.20%; reported gross revenue and customer incentives reproduce the arithmetic bridge. BPS data show national e-commerce transaction value rising 17.08% from 2023 to 2024 while the non-marketplace component accounts for about 98.46% of the nominal increase. Bank Indonesia reports 34.4693 billion mobile-and-internet digital-payment transactions in 2024, up 36.1%, but those payment traces are not sales. PMK 37/2025 provides a legal bridge from marketplace records to reporting/withholding duties, while operational matching and outcomes remain separate empirical questions. The contribution is cross-record empirical reconciliation, not a residual estimate of hidden GDP, income, or unpaid tax.

## Citation / disclosure rule for journal submission

If the revised SSRN version remains public, disclose it according to the journal's preprint policy. Do not describe the old residual version as an independent prior paper if the journal submission is its direct successor. If the old version remains accessible through SSRN version history, the manuscript or cover letter can state that the public preprint underwent a fundamental revision and that the current version supersedes its previous central quantitative interpretation.

## Do not

- delete the historical record merely to hide the correction;
- leave the old abstract as the prominent current abstract while submitting the new paper;
- claim that the old quantitative results remain “robust” if the current design rejects their interpretation;
- submit both old and new Invisible Ledger manuscripts as separate original papers with overlapping evidence.
