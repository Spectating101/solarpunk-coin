# Invisible Ledger — Primary Venue Route

## Primary route: Electronic Commerce Research and Applications (Elsevier)

**Route status:** strongest current scope fit among checked outlets; verify the current Guide for Authors immediately before submission.

## Why this route

Electronic Commerce Research and Applications explicitly describes e-commerce as multidisciplinary and includes:

- digital economy and digital transformation;
- electronic payment systems;
- exchanges and electronic marketplaces;
- public policy and legal issues;
- industry studies and case analysis;
- empirical studies of e-commerce problems.

The current Invisible Ledger article is exactly a cross-record e-commerce case study spanning platform accounting, national e-commerce statistics, payment traces, and an administrative visibility boundary.

This is a better fit than a general economics journal that would likely expect a larger causal or population-identification design.

## Editorial positioning

Do **not** pitch the paper as a macro hidden-economy estimate.

Lead with:

> The same digital-commercial transformation can produce materially different signals in platform accounting, national e-commerce statistics, payment-system records, and administrative information. The paper provides a source-auditable Indonesia reconciliation of those records and explains selected divergences without aggregating non-equivalent measures.

The Tokopedia audited mechanism should appear in the first paragraph of the cover letter because it demonstrates that the paper contains a concrete empirical result rather than only a conceptual measurement framework.

## Compact route abstract — 124 words

> Digital commerce is recorded through platform metrics and revenue, national e-commerce statistics, payment systems, and administrative records, but these sources measure different objects. This paper reconciles those records in Indonesia. Audited GoTo disclosures show Tokopedia e-commerce GTV falling 8.90% from FY2022 to FY2023 while third-party net segment revenue rises 53.20%; reported gross revenue and incentives reproduce the accounting bridge. BPS data show national e-commerce transaction value rising 17.08% in 2024, with 98.46% of the nominal increase outside the marketplace component. Bank Indonesia records 34.4693 billion mobile-and-internet payment transactions, up 36.1%, but these are not sales. PMK 37/2025 then illustrates the distinction between private digital records, legal reportability, operational activation, and unobserved matching or outcomes. The contribution is cross-record empirical reconciliation, not a hidden-economy aggregate.

## Cover-letter emphasis

Recommended core:

> The manuscript addresses a measurement problem that is central to e-commerce research: platform operating metrics, platform revenue, national e-commerce statistics and digital-payment records are frequently discussed as proxies for the same transformation even though they measure different objects. Its core mechanism case uses audited GoTo/Tokopedia disclosures to reconcile a period in which e-commerce GTV declines 8.90% while third-party net segment revenue rises 53.20%, then examines where broader national growth appears in BPS channel statistics and how that differs from Bank Indonesia payment traces.

## Route risks

### 1. “Too many modules”

The manuscript must read as one e-commerce measurement question, not four mini-papers. Keep the observation-layer figure early.

### 2. “Single issuer anecdote”

The Tokopedia case is a mechanism case, not the population estimate. BPS/BI supply national observation layers but do not turn it into a causal platform study.

### 3. “This distinction is already in measurement handbooks”

Concede that. The novelty is the audited Indonesia empirical sequence, not the abstract fact that transaction value, fees, and payment traces differ.

### 4. Time-sensitive PMK material

Refresh the institutional section immediately before submission and on/after 2026-11-01 if operational status changes.
