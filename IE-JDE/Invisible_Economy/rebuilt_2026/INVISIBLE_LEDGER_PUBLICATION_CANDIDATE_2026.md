# The Invisible Ledger

## Reconciling Digital Commerce Across Corporate, Statistical, Payment, and Administrative Records in Indonesia

Christopher Ongko (王新福)  
Yuan Ze University  
Publication candidate · September 2026

## Abstract

Digital commerce is often summarized with a single large number: platform gross transaction value, corporate revenue, national e-commerce sales, payment-system transactions, or tax records. These quantities are related, but they are not interchangeable. This paper develops the **Invisible Ledger** as a cross-record reconciliation framework for studying what becomes visible when the same digital-commercial transformation passes through different accounting, statistical, payment, and administrative systems.

Indonesia provides a useful setting because several observation layers can be examined without forcing them into a common monetary aggregate. At the issuer layer, audited GoTo disclosures show that Tokopedia e-commerce gross transaction value fell 8.90% from FY2022 to FY2023 while third-party net segment revenue rose 53.20%. Audited segment accounts reconcile that divergence arithmetically: gross third-party revenue increased by Rp845.7 billion while customer incentives declined by Rp1.299 trillion, with the incentive reduction accounting for 60.56% of the arithmetic increase in third-party net segment revenue. The result is a mechanism example of economic translation, not evidence of hidden GDP or a latent tax base.

At the national-statistical layer, BPS data show e-commerce transaction value rising from Rp1,100.87 trillion in 2023 to Rp1,288.93 trillion in 2024, or 17.08%. Marketplace value rose only from Rp200.68 trillion to Rp203.58 trillion, while the non-marketplace component rose from Rp900.19 trillion to Rp1,085.35 trillion. On this decomposition, about 98.46% of the nominal increase occurred outside the marketplace component. BPS separately reports that the estimated number of e-commerce businesses increased 15.30% in 2024. A revised BPS analysis also reports that 28.63% of marketplace-using businesses had financial statements, compared with 12.25% among non-marketplace businesses. These relationships are descriptive and do not establish causal formalization.

At the payment layer, Bank Indonesia reports 34.4693 billion mobile-application and internet digital-payment transactions in 2024, up 36.1%, demonstrating a large and rapidly changing transaction trace that is definitionally distinct from e-commerce sales. At the administrative layer, PMK 37/2025 creates a legal bridge from marketplace records and seller identifiers to withholding and reporting duties, but implementation has been postponed through 31 October 2026, with activation scheduled for 1 November 2026. Matching, verified administrative use, and compliance effects therefore remain unobserved as of the paper's currentness date.

The contribution is not a new aggregate estimate of an “unmeasured economy.” It is a disciplined empirical account of **economic translation across records**. The paper shows how different observation layers can imply materially different accounts of growth while remaining internally valid for their own purposes, and why reconciliation is necessary before one record is used as a proxy for another.

**Keywords:** digital economy; platforms; e-commerce; accounting comparability; digital payments; statistical measurement; third-party reporting; Indonesia; economic measurement

## 1. Introduction

Digital commerce produces many records before it produces one coherent economic interpretation. A marketplace records transactions and recognizes revenue according to its accounting perimeter. Participating businesses report sales and business characteristics through statistical surveys. Payment systems record transfers and settlement activity. Tax rules determine which records become legally reportable and whether public authorities can use them for administration. National accounts then measure production and value added under yet another set of definitions.

These records are connected, but they do not measure the same object.

That distinction matters because a visible digital metric can easily become a proxy for a broader economic process. Platform gross transaction value may be read as though it were platform revenue. Platform revenue may be treated as a summary of all commerce coordinated by the platform. Payment-system growth can be described as though it were equivalent to sales growth. Marketplace activity can be mistaken for the whole e-commerce population. A transaction-to-revenue residual can be misread as unmeasured GDP or untaxed income.

The earlier Invisible Ledger project made precisely that last mistake. It treated the difference between platform-mediated transaction value and platform revenue too aggressively as a residual “invisible economy.” That interpretation does not survive accounting and national-measurement scrutiny. Gross transaction value is not merchant income, value added, GDP, taxable income, or unpaid tax; platform revenue is not the seller's turnover; and payment flows are not one-for-one measures of commerce.

The correction does not eliminate the research problem. It reveals a better one.

> **When digital-commercial activity grows, where does that growth become visible, why can different records imply different accounts of change, and what additional economic structure can be recovered by reconciling those records?**

This paper answers that question through four observation layers in Indonesia:

1. **issuer accounting**, using Tokopedia/GoTo to examine how facilitated activity translates into recognized revenue;
2. **national e-commerce statistics**, using BPS to examine total value, channel composition, business participation, and business characteristics;
3. **payment-system traces**, using Bank Indonesia to separate transaction infrastructure from commerce measures;
4. **administrative visibility**, using PMK 37/2025 and Directorate General of Taxes implementation material to distinguish record existence from legally activated reporting, matching, verified use, and outcomes.

The paper calls the fuller account recoverable from the relationships and disagreements among these partial records the **Invisible Ledger**. The term does not describe a hidden stock of money. It describes a reconciliation problem: economic activity passes through multiple observation systems, and information is lost or transformed when one layer is mistaken for another.

The empirical strategy is deliberately modular. The paper does not add issuer GTV, BPS transaction value, payment volume, or tax records into a common total. It does not estimate an “ASEAN invisible economy,” infer missing GDP, or treat a marketplace residual as a tax base. Each module retains its source-native unit and definition. Cross-record comparison is used only when it changes an economically relevant interpretation and when the relationship can be stated without false equivalence.

Three findings emerge.

First, **issuer activity and issuer revenue can produce opposite longitudinal growth signals**. Tokopedia's FY2022-FY2023 e-commerce GTV fell 8.90%, while audited third-party net segment revenue rose 53.20%. The segment accounts show how changes in gross revenue and customer incentives reconcile that divergence.

Second, **national digital-commercial growth is not exhausted by major marketplace records**. BPS transaction value rose 17.08% from 2023 to 2024, but marketplace value rose only 1.45%, while the non-marketplace component rose 20.57%. Almost all of the nominal increase in the published BPS total occurred outside the marketplace component. BPS also reports 15.30% growth in the estimated number of e-commerce businesses, and its revised business analysis shows large descriptive differences in financial-statement ownership between marketplace and non-marketplace businesses.

Third, **digital traceability is not the same as institutional usability**. Bank Indonesia records tens of billions of digital-payment transactions, but those records are payment-system observations rather than e-commerce sales. PMK 37/2025 goes further by creating a legal architecture connecting marketplace records, seller identifiers, turnover, withholding, and reporting. Yet the policy's operational activation is postponed through October 2026. The existence of the legal bridge therefore cannot be treated as evidence that matching, verified use, or compliance effects have already occurred.

The contribution is consequently methodological and empirical. Existing research explains platform economics, accounting presentation, digital-economy measurement, business formalization, digital footprints, and third-party information. This paper connects those literatures through a source-auditable sequence that asks what happens to the economic interpretation as the observation layer changes.

## 2. Literature and conceptual framework

### 2.1 Facilitated activity and platform revenue

Multi-sided-platform theory provides the first foundation. Platforms coordinate interactions among participant groups while choosing price structures that need not move proportionally with gross transaction activity (Rochet and Tirole 2003; Caillaud and Jullien 2003; Armstrong 2006). Network effects and cross-side pricing allow one side of the market to be subsidized while monetization occurs through commissions, advertising, logistics, payments, subscriptions, or other services (Parker and Van Alstyne 2005). Hagiu and Wright (2015) further distinguish multi-sided platforms from resellers and vertically integrated firms by the control retained by affiliated participants.

These models imply that facilitated transaction value and platform revenue are related but non-equivalent. More importantly for this paper, they need not provide the same **growth signal**. Changes in take rates, incentives, advertising intensity, logistics services, product mix, or business perimeter can cause recognized revenue to move differently from transaction activity.

### 2.2 Accounting perimeter and comparability

Revenue recognition provides the second foundation. Under IFRS 15, principal-versus-agent considerations affect whether an entity recognizes gross consideration or a net amount for arranging the provision of goods or services. Incentives, consolidation, business mix, and presentation therefore matter for how commercial activity becomes accounting revenue.

Financial-statement comparability research shows why translation across accounting systems matters for users of information. De Franco, Kothari, and Verdi (2011) link greater comparability to a stronger information environment for analysts. This paper does not estimate analyst behavior, but applies the same underlying principle: when transaction activity and recognized revenue tell different stories, the accounting translation itself may contain useful economic information.

### 2.3 Digital-economy measurement

Digital-economy measurement literature establishes the boundary that invalidates the original residual interpretation. Ahmad and Schreyer (2016) show that digitalization creates real measurement challenges but does not make gross transaction flows an alternative definition of GDP. Byrne, Fernald, and Reinsdorf (2016) similarly caution against using digital mismeasurement as a universal explanation for macroeconomic puzzles. The IMF (2018) highlights classification, cross-border activity, prices, and intermediation as specific digital-economy measurement challenges.

Recent international statistical frameworks sharpen the distinction. The OECD Digital Supply and Use Table framework separates the nature of the transaction, the product, and the producing industry. The second edition of the *Handbook on Measuring Digital Trade* distinguishes digitally ordered transactions from the intermediation services supplied by digital platforms (IMF, OECD, UNCTAD, and WTO 2023).

The implication is central to this paper: **difference is not missingness by default**. A platform's transaction metric, revenue measure, merchant sale, payment transfer, and national-account value-added measure can differ because they answer different questions.

### 2.4 Business participation and digital footprints

A third literature moves beyond the platform. Small firms vary substantially in recordkeeping, financial planning, organizational form, and formalization (McKenzie and Woodruff 2017; La Porta and Shleifer 2014; Ulyssea 2018). Digital footprints can also contain economically useful information where conventional records are thin (Berg et al. 2020).

This motivates the BPS layer. Marketplace participation may be associated with different recordkeeping or payment practices even if marketplace adoption does not causally formalize a firm. Likewise, national e-commerce growth may occur through social media, messaging applications, or other channels not summarized by the largest marketplace operators.

### 2.5 Third-party information and institutional visibility

The final literature concerns the conversion of private records into public administrative information. Kleven et al. (2011) show that income subject to third-party reporting is much harder to evade than purely self-reported income in Danish audit data. Pomeranz (2015) demonstrates the enforcement value of VAT paper trails. Slemrod (2019) reviews information reporting and remittance as central instruments of tax enforcement. OECD platform-reporting rules similarly require covered operators to collect and report seller identity and consideration information.

The important distinction for the Invisible Ledger is between **traceability and usability**. A digital record can exist without being legally reportable. A reporting duty can exist without evidence of transmission. Transmission can occur without successful matching. Matching can occur without verified administrative use or a measured outcome.

The institutional visibility chain used here is therefore:

```text
record exists
    ↓
actor holds record
    ↓
legal reporting / withholding duty
    ↓
usable identifiers
    ↓
transmission
    ↓
matching
    ↓
verified administrative use
    ↓
observed outcome
```

No later stage is inferred from an earlier one.

## 3. Research design and evidence discipline

### 3.1 Reconciliation rather than aggregation

The paper uses a **modular reconciliation design**. Each evidence module retains its own unit, perimeter, and authority:

| Module | Primary object | Main question |
|---|---|---|
| GoTo/Tokopedia | GTV and segment revenue | Can activity and recognized revenue give different growth signals, and can the accounting bridge be reconciled? |
| BPS e-commerce | estimated transaction value, channels, businesses | Where does broader national digital-commercial growth appear? |
| Bank Indonesia | payment-system transactions | What transaction traces exist outside sales/accounting records? |
| PMK 37 / DJP | seller identity, turnover, withholding/reporting architecture | When do private transaction records become legally administrable information? |

The modules are not pooled into a common regression or added into one monetary aggregate.

### 3.2 Source hierarchy

The evidence package prioritizes:

1. audited issuer annual reports and source-native operating metrics;
2. BPS national statistical publications and revised BPS analytical releases;
3. Bank Indonesia official reports and statistical tables;
4. Ministry of Finance regulations and Directorate General of Taxes implementation notices;
5. official-government cross-checks where the statistical publication is not directly exposed at row level in the accessible archive.

Every headline quantitative result has a reproduction-map entry tying the manuscript claim to source-controlled rows and a transformation rule.

### 3.3 Admission and exclusion rules

A number enters the headline argument only if its unit and interpretation are stable enough for the intended comparison. The paper explicitly excludes or demotes:

- the old $185 billion ASEAN “invisible economy” calibration;
- the 12.3× “fiscal multiplier”;
- transaction value minus platform revenue as missing GDP, participant income, taxable income, or unpaid tax;
- assumption-dependent country allocations of regional Grab or Shopee metrics as headline Indonesia evidence;
- an exact 2024 BPS business count where the accessible primary-table representation remains unresolved;
- implied transaction value per business derived from that unresolved count;
- a single 2024 QRIS growth rate because two official Bank Indonesia publications expose different vintage values;
- PMK 37 matching, use, or compliance effects that have not yet been observed.

The result is intentionally narrower than earlier versions of the project.

## 4. Issuer growth translation: Tokopedia FY2022-FY2023

### 4.1 Opposite growth signals

GoTo's 2023 disclosures provide a direct example of why a transaction measure and recognized revenue should not be treated as interchangeable summaries of platform performance.

E-commerce GTV fell from Rp273.146 trillion in 2022 to Rp248.836 trillion in 2023, a decline of **8.90%**. Over the same period, audited third-party net segment revenue increased from Rp4.031 trillion to Rp6.175 trillion, or **53.20%**.

### Table 1. Tokopedia/GoTo e-commerce translation, FY2022-FY2023

| Measure | FY2022 | FY2023 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| E-commerce GTV | Rp273.146tn | Rp248.836tn | **-8.90%** |
| Third-party gross segment revenue | Rp8.143tn | Rp8.989tn | +10.38% |
| Customer incentives | Rp4.112tn | Rp2.814tn | **-31.58%** |
| Third-party net segment revenue | Rp4.031tn | Rp6.175tn | **+53.20%** |

A transaction-centered observer would describe contraction. A net-revenue-centered observer would describe rapid expansion. Neither source-native measure is inherently wrong. They record different parts of the commercial and accounting process.

### 4.2 Audited accounting reconciliation

The audited segment table also allows the divergence to be reconciled without inventing an economic residual.

Third-party gross segment revenue increased by Rp845.670 billion. Customer incentives declined by Rp1.299 trillion. Third-party net segment revenue increased by Rp2.144 trillion.

The arithmetic identity is exact:

```text
increase in gross third-party revenue      Rp0.846tn
+ reduction in customer incentives         Rp1.299tn
----------------------------------------------------
= increase in net third-party revenue      Rp2.144tn
```

The reduction in incentives therefore represents **60.56% of the arithmetic increase in net revenue**:

```text
(4.112320 - 2.813719) / (6.175190 - 4.030919) = 60.56%
```

This is an accounting bridge, not a causal decomposition. It does not show why GoTo changed incentives, what caused demand, whether profitability improved because of the incentive change, or how merchant income moved. It establishes something narrower and cleaner: much of the opposite GTV/net-revenue signal can be understood from source-native changes inside the reported accounting translation.

That mechanism result is more informative than the old transaction-minus-revenue residual. Instead of labeling the difference “invisible,” it explains how a platform can coordinate less measured transaction value while recognizing substantially more net segment revenue.

## 5. Where broader e-commerce growth appears

Issuer accounts answer a company question. BPS answers a different question: what is happening across the estimated national population of e-commerce businesses and channels?

### 5.1 National transaction value and business participation

BPS reports e-commerce transaction value of Rp1,100.87 trillion in 2023. The 2024 publication, cross-checked in an official Ministry of Trade analysis based on the BPS survey, reports Rp1,288.93 trillion in 2024. The increase is **17.08%**.

BPS also states that the estimated number of e-commerce businesses increased **15.30%** in 2024 relative to the prior year. This paper uses that official growth statement but does not publish an implied value-per-business estimate because the exact national count row has not been frozen to the same source-control standard as the transaction-value series.

The distinction matters. Aggregate transaction growth and business-count growth represent different margins of expansion. The current evidence establishes that both increased substantially; it does not require a potentially fragile denominator calculation to make that point.

### 5.2 Marketplace and non-marketplace channels

The channel decomposition changes the interpretation more sharply.

BPS reports Rp200.68 trillion of 2023 e-commerce transaction value through marketplaces/digital platforms and Rp900.19 trillion outside that marketplace component. For 2024, the official-government cross-check of BPS data reports Rp203.58 trillion through marketplaces, representing 15.79% of the total; the corresponding non-marketplace component is Rp1,085.35 trillion, or 84.21%.

### Table 2. BPS e-commerce transaction-value anatomy

| Channel | 2023 | 2024 | Growth |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Marketplace | Rp200.68tn | Rp203.58tn | **+1.45%** |
| Non-marketplace | Rp900.19tn | Rp1,085.35tn | **+20.57%** |
| Total | Rp1,100.87tn | Rp1,288.93tn | **+17.08%** |

The total nominal increase is Rp188.06 trillion. The non-marketplace component increases by Rp185.16 trillion. Consequently, **98.46% of the nominal increase in the published total is arithmetically attributable to the non-marketplace component**.

That is a descriptive decomposition, not a causal attribution. It does not establish that non-marketplace channels are more productive, more informal, less taxable, or welfare-superior. It establishes that a marketplace-centered observation layer would provide a very different account of where the published national increase occurred.

This is the core Invisible Ledger point in a national setting: the apparent location of growth depends on which economic record is being observed.

### 5.3 Business characteristics are associated with observation channel

BPS's revised analysis of marketplace use adds a participant-side layer. Among the businesses in the published comparison, **28.63% of marketplace businesses reported having financial statements, compared with 12.25% of non-marketplace businesses**. Marketplace businesses also show higher reported participation in ICT-utilization training: 8.49% versus 2.83%.

These differences are economically relevant because recordkeeping affects what can become visible to lenders, platforms, tax authorities, and statistical systems. But they are observational. Marketplace users may differ from non-marketplace businesses in size, sector, management quality, location, digital capability, or other dimensions. The evidence does not identify marketplace adoption as the cause of financial-statement ownership or formalization.

The result is therefore bounded: different commercial channels are associated with different recordkeeping profiles, reinforcing the need to distinguish channel composition from a universal notion of “the digital business.”

## 6. Payment-system traces are a different observation layer

Bank Indonesia adds another representation of digital activity.

In its Q4-2024 Monetary Policy Report, Bank Indonesia defines the cited digital-payments aggregate as transactions through **mobile applications and the internet**. The 2024 Economic Report records **34.4693 billion** such transactions in 2024, an increase of **36.1%** from 2023. Mobile-application transaction volume reached 32.1826 billion and grew 39.1%, while internet transaction volume reached 2.2867 billion and grew 4.4%.

These growth rates are materially different from the 17.08% increase in BPS e-commerce transaction value. That difference should not be interpreted as inconsistency requiring one series to be “corrected.” The measures have different denominators, units, transaction populations, and purposes.

A payment transaction can be related to commerce without being a sale. Payment-system series can include transfers and payment activity that cannot be mapped one-for-one into merchant revenue, platform GTV, national e-commerce transaction value, income, output, or GDP. Conversely, e-commerce transactions can use cash or payment arrangements that do not map cleanly into the selected Bank Indonesia series.

The analytical contribution of the payment layer is therefore not another measure of “digital economy size.” It is evidence that digitalization generates abundant transactional traces whose growth can diverge from sales measures because they observe a different economic process.

The source-control exercise also reveals why publication vintage matters. Bank Indonesia's Q4-2024 Monetary Policy Report and its later 2024 Economic Report expose different headline QRIS growth figures. Rather than choose one silently, this paper excludes a single QRIS growth rate from the headline argument. The disagreement is itself a reminder that even official series require definition and vintage control before cross-record comparison.

## 7. From private records to institutional visibility

The final layer asks when privately generated digital records become administratively usable.

### 7.1 PMK 37/2025 creates a legal bridge

Indonesia's PMK 37/2025 establishes an architecture under which designated marketplaces can be assigned PPh Article 22 withholding, remittance, and reporting responsibilities for covered domestic sellers. The legal design connects marketplace-mediated commerce to seller identifiers and turnover information rather than treating platform corporate revenue as the seller's tax base.

This is a conceptually important shift in observation. A marketplace record is not merely evidence that a transaction occurred. Under the legal architecture, specified seller and transaction information can become part of a fiscal reporting path.

The distinction can be represented as:

```text
private transaction record
        ↓
seller / account identity
        ↓
legally specified reporting or withholding duty
        ↓
transmission to public authority
        ↓
matching to taxpayer
        ↓
verified administrative use
        ↓
compliance / revenue / behavioural outcome
```

### 7.2 Legal architecture is not operational outcome

The current implementation history demonstrates why those stages must remain separate.

DJP initially announced four marketplace appointments on 1 July 2026. On 5 August 2026, however, DJP announced that implementation of PMK 37/2025 would be postponed through **31 October 2026**, with PPh Article 22 collection scheduled to begin on **1 November 2026**. The announcement states that the prior appointment decisions will be cancelled and marketplaces reappointed, and that PPh already collected under the earlier appointment actions is to be returned to domestic sellers.

As of the currentness date of this manuscript, 14 September 2026, the defensible institutional statement is therefore:

- the legal architecture exists;
- an initial appointment action occurred;
- operational collection under the revised schedule is not yet active;
- matching evidence is unknown;
- verified administrative use is unknown;
- compliance effects are unknown.

This is a stronger research result than treating the July appointment announcement as proof of functioning administrative integration. The temporal sequence reveals the difference between **legal visibility** and **operational visibility**.

It also creates a future empirical opportunity. Once implementation actually begins, subsequent work can test whether the legal bridge produces observable transmission, matching, correction, and compliance outcomes. Those are future stages, not assumptions embedded in the current paper.

## 8. Synthesis: what the Invisible Ledger recovers

The four modules can now be interpreted as a sequence of economic translation.

### Table 3. Observation layers and what they reveal

| Layer | What is observed | What changes the interpretation | What cannot be inferred alone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Issuer | GTV, gross revenue, incentives, net revenue | monetization/accounting translation | merchant income, GDP, tax base |
| BPS | national transaction value, channels, businesses | participation and channel composition | issuer performance, causal formalization |
| BI | payment transactions | payment-infrastructure intensity | sales, output, platform GTV |
| Tax administration | identifiers, legal duties, reporting architecture | institutional ability to request/use private records | successful matching or policy effect before observed |

The paper's empirical findings are not one grand total. They are a set of disciplined disagreements.

Tokopedia demonstrates that transaction activity and recognized revenue can move in opposite directions, with audited accounting components explaining the arithmetic bridge. BPS demonstrates that national e-commerce growth can occur overwhelmingly outside the marketplace component even when marketplace firms remain highly visible in public discussion. Bank Indonesia demonstrates that payment traces can grow much faster than e-commerce transaction value because they observe a broader payment-system process. PMK 37 demonstrates that abundant private records do not become public administrative information merely because they exist; law, activation, identifiers, transmission, and matching are separate stages.

The **Invisible Ledger** is the economic structure recoverable from those relationships.

This is different from claiming that conventional statistics are “wrong.” Each record is produced for a different purpose. Platform accounting follows the issuer perimeter. BPS estimates business activity. Bank Indonesia records payment-system transactions. Tax administration defines legally relevant information. The problem arises when a partial record is used as a proxy for another economic object without reconciliation.

A useful decision rule follows. Cross-record divergence deserves investigation when four conditions coincide:

1. one record is being used as a proxy for another;
2. the records imply materially different conclusions;
3. a financial, policy, statistical, or administrative decision depends on the distinction;
4. the divergence can be investigated through source-native definitions or mechanisms.

That rule prevents the Invisible Ledger from becoming a doctrine that all datasets should agree. It makes disagreement informative only where the interpretation changes.

## 9. Limitations and research agenda

The paper remains bounded in several important ways.

First, the issuer mechanism is a **case study**, not a population estimate for Indonesian platforms. The Tokopedia result is valuable because it is direct and auditable, not because it statistically represents every marketplace.

Second, the BPS channel analysis is descriptive. It does not identify the cause of marketplace/non-marketplace growth differences. The official 15.30% business-count growth statement is retained, but an exact implied transaction value per business is intentionally omitted until the exact count table is frozen.

Third, BPS business-characteristic comparisons are observational. Differences in financial-statement ownership or ICT training cannot be interpreted as causal effects of marketplace participation.

Fourth, payment-system traces cannot be mapped one-for-one into sales. Their role is to show a distinct observation system, not to create a new estimate of economic output.

Fifth, PMK 37 is a moving institutional object. The current manuscript freezes its state as of 14 September 2026. The scheduled 1 November 2026 activation must be rechecked before any later submission or revision. The current evidence does not establish matching, verified use, or compliance outcomes.

Sixth, the paper focuses empirically on Indonesia. ASEAN and global issuer histories may provide external corroboration of recurring measurement relationships, but they are not pooled into the core estimates.

These boundaries suggest a clear research agenda: expand the direct issuer-transition sample only where definitions and reporting perimeters are stable; track channel composition over additional BPS waves; build source-controlled payment definitions over time; and evaluate administrative matching only after implementation produces observable records.

## 10. Conclusion

Digital commerce does not create one ledger. It creates several.

A platform records transaction activity and recognizes revenue through an accounting perimeter. Businesses sell through marketplaces, social media, messaging applications, and other channels captured differently by statistical systems. Payment infrastructure records transfers at a different unit and scale. Public institutions can use only the information that legal and operational systems make administratively available.

Those records can all be valid while telling different stories.

Tokopedia's FY2022-FY2023 case makes the problem concrete: GTV fell 8.90% while audited third-party net segment revenue rose 53.20%. The accounting bridge explains that divergence without inventing a hidden residual. BPS then changes the observation layer again: national e-commerce transaction value rose 17.08% in 2024, while marketplace value grew only 1.45% and the non-marketplace component grew 20.57%. Bank Indonesia records 34.4693 billion mobile-and-internet digital-payment transactions growing 36.1%, but those are payment traces rather than sales. PMK 37 finally demonstrates that even rich private records require legal activation and operational transmission before they become administratively usable.

The Invisible Ledger is therefore not an estimate of money missing from GDP. It is a **method of economic reconciliation across observation boundaries**.

That reframing produces a more modest claim than the project's earlier residual narrative, but a more durable one: **the apparent scale, direction, composition, and institutional visibility of digital-commercial growth can change materially with the record being observed, and those differences can reveal economic structure when their definitions and mechanisms are reconciled.**
