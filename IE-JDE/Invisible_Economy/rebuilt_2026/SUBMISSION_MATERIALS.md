## Standard abstract

Digital commerce is described through transaction value, platform revenue, national e-commerce statistics, payment-system activity, and administrative records. These quantities are related but do not measure the same economic object. This paper develops the **Invisible Ledger** as a source-auditable reconciliation of those partial records in Indonesia.

Audited GoTo disclosures show that Tokopedia e-commerce GTV fell 8.90% from FY2022 to FY2023 while third-party net segment revenue rose 53.20%. Audited segment components reconcile the sign reversal: gross third-party revenue increased by Rp845.7 billion while customer incentives declined by Rp1.299 trillion, with the incentive reduction accounting for 60.56% of the arithmetic net-revenue increase. BPS data then show national e-commerce transaction value rising 17.08% from 2023 to 2024 while marketplace value rose only 1.45% and the non-marketplace component rose 20.57%; 98.46% of the nominal increase occurred in the non-marketplace component. BPS separately reports 15.30% growth in the estimated number of e-commerce businesses. Bank Indonesia reports 34.4693 billion mobile-and-internet digital-payment transactions in 2024, up 36.1%, but those payment traces are definitionally distinct from sales. Finally, PMK 37/2025 creates a legal marketplace reporting/withholding architecture, while operational activation is postponed through 31 October 2026 and matching, verified use, and outcomes remain unobserved.

The contribution is not a new aggregate estimate of an “unmeasured economy.” International measurement guidance already distinguishes platform intermediation fees, underlying transactions, payment data, and other partial data sources. The paper's narrower contribution is the **Indonesia empirical reconciliation sequence**: an audited issuer mechanism, national channel anatomy, controlled payment definitions, and staged institutional visibility without aggregating non-equivalent records.

## Short abstract

This paper examines how the interpretation of Indonesia's digital-commercial growth changes when the observation layer changes. Tokopedia's audited FY2022-FY2023 data show e-commerce GTV falling 8.90% while third-party net segment revenue rises 53.20%, with reported gross revenue and incentives reconciling the arithmetic divergence. BPS then shows national e-commerce transaction value rising 17.08% from 2023 to 2024, with 98.46% of the nominal increase outside the marketplace component. Bank Indonesia records 34.4693 billion mobile-and-internet digital-payment transactions in 2024, but those traces are not sales. PMK 37/2025 creates a legal bridge from marketplace records to tax reporting while matching/use/outcomes remain unobserved. The contribution is a source-auditable reconciliation across partial records, not a hidden-economy aggregate or a claim that gross transaction value equals GDP, income, or taxable base.

## Contribution statement

The paper provides a source-auditable Indonesia case study of how digital-commercial growth changes interpretation across four observation layers: issuer accounting, national e-commerce statistics, payment-system traces, and administrative reporting architecture. It contributes an audited mechanism reconciliation for a sign-reversing Tokopedia GTV/net-revenue episode and integrates that result with BPS channel anatomy, Bank Indonesia payment-definition controls, and a staged institutional-visibility analysis.

The paper does not claim originality for the facts that platform revenue differs from underlying transaction value or that company reports/payment data provide partial views of e-commerce. International statistical guidance already establishes those distinctions. The contribution is the empirical sequence, mechanism evidence, and disciplined rule for when cross-record divergence becomes economically decision-relevant.

---

## Methods statement

The study uses modular cross-record reconciliation rather than aggregation. Each module retains its source-native unit, perimeter, and definition. Audited issuer evidence is used for the Tokopedia mechanism; BPS publications for national transaction/channel and business-characteristic evidence; Bank Indonesia reports for payment traces; and Ministry of Finance/DJP sources for institutional visibility.

No module is treated as a substitute for another. The study does not add GTV, BPS transaction value, payment volumes, or tax records into a common total. Quantitative headline results map to controlled source rows and transformations in `RESULT_REPRODUCTION_MAP.csv`.

## Ethics / data statement

The study uses public issuer filings, official statistics, central-bank publications, laws, and tax-administration notices. It does not use individual-level taxpayer or private transaction data. The paper's institutional section distinguishes the existence of private records from legal authority to obtain/use them.

## Plain-language summary

A digital economy can look very different depending on which record you open. A platform may report lower transaction activity but higher revenue. National statistics may show most e-commerce growth happening outside major marketplaces. Payment systems can grow faster than e-commerce sales because they record a broader set of transactions. Tax rules can make private transaction records legally reportable without proving that authorities have already matched or used them. This paper follows those different records in Indonesia and asks what each one actually measures. The main lesson is not that one dataset is wrong. It is that economic interpretation becomes unreliable when one partial record is used as a substitute for another without reconciling their definitions and mechanisms.

Tokopedia's GTV fell about 9% in 2023 while audited third-party net revenue rose 53%. BPS says national e-commerce value then rose 17% in 2024, but almost all of the nominal increase occurred outside its marketplace component. Bank Indonesia records digital-payment volume growing 36%, but that is a different object again. *Invisible Ledger* asks what each record measures, why the signals diverge, and what changes when private transaction records become legally reportable. The paper replaces an earlier hidden-economy residual story with a source-auditable reconciliation framework.
